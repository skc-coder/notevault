> [!theorem]
> **Election Balance Equation**
> Let total registered voters be $T$[cite: 1].
> 1. $\text{Voted (Turnout)} = T - \text{Non-voters}$[cite: 1].
> 2. $\text{Valid Votes} = \text{Turnout} - \text{Invalid Votes}$[cite: 1].
> 3. $\text{Valid Votes} = V_{\text{winner}} + V_{\text{loser}}$[cite: 1].
> 4. $\text{Margin of Victory} = V_{\text{winner}} - V_{\text{loser}}$[cite: 1].

> [!question]
> **Direct Two-Candidate Election (Q7)**
> In an election between two candidates, one candidate got $56\%$ votes and won the election by $600$ votes[cite: 1]. Find the total number of voters in the list[cite: 1].
> - A. $6500$
> - B. $5000$
> - C. $2500$
> - D. $7700$[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> Let valid/total votes be $x$[cite: 1].
> Winner gets $56\%$ of $x \implies$ Loser gets $(100 - 56)\% = 44\%$ of $x$[cite: 1].
> $$\text{Margin} = (56\% - 44\%) \text{ of } x = 12\% \text{ of } x$$[cite: 1]
> $$12\% \text{ of } x = 600 \implies x = \frac{600}{12} \times 100 = 5000$$[cite: 1]
> **Correct Option: B**[cite: 1]

> [!question]
> **Three-Candidate Ratio Election**
> Arjun, Bhaskar, and Saral contested an election[cite: 1]. Arjun got $50\%$ votes more than Saral, and Saral got $20\%$ votes less than Bhaskar[cite: 1]. The difference between the votes of Bhaskar and Saral is $1296$[cite: 1]. What is half of the difference between the votes of Arjun and Bhaskar?[cite: 1]
> - (A) $13988$
> - (B) $7766$
> - (C) $3888$
> - (D) $648$[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> 1. Formulate ratios:
>    Let Bhaskar's votes $= 100$ units[cite: 1].
>    Saral gets $20\%$ less than Bhaskar $= 80$ units[cite: 1].
>    Arjun gets $50\%$ more than Saral:
>    $$\text{Arjun} = 80 \times \left(1 + \frac{50}{100}\right) = 80 \times \frac{3}{2} = 120\text{ units}$$[cite: 1]
> 2. Ratio $A : B : S$:
>    $$A : B : S = 120 : 100 : 80 = 6 : 5 : 4$$[cite: 1]
>    Let $A = 6k$, $B = 5k$, $S = 4k$[cite: 1].
> 3. Evaluate unit values:
>    $$\text{Diff}(B, S) = 5k - 4k = k = 1296$$[cite: 1]
> 4. Half of the difference between Arjun and Bhaskar:
>    $$\text{Diff}(A, B) = 6k - 5k = k$$[cite: 1]
>    $$\frac{\text{Diff}(A, B)}{2} = \frac{k}{2} = \frac{1296}{2} = 648$$[cite: 1]
> **Correct Option: (C) / 648**[cite: 1]

> [!question]
> **Election with Non-Voters and Invalid Ballots**
> In an election between two candidates, $10\%$ of voters did not vote, and $150$ votes were declared invalid[cite: 1]. The winner got $350$ votes more than his opponent, thereby securing $50\%$ of the total registered voters in the voter list[cite: 1]. Find the percentage of votes scored by the defeated candidate out of the total registered votes[cite: 1].
> - (A) $28.5\%$
> - (B) $30.2\%$
> - (C) $32.5\%$
> - (D) $25.6\%$[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> 1. Let total registered voters $= x$[cite: 1].
>    - Turnout $= 90\% \text{ of } x$[cite: 1].
>    - Valid votes $= 90\% \text{ of } x - 150$[cite: 1].
>    - Winner votes $= 50\% \text{ of } x$[cite: 1].
> 2. Defeated candidate votes:
>    $$\text{Loser} = \text{Valid Votes} - \text{Winner} = (90\%x - 150) - 50\%x = 40\%x - 150$$[cite: 1]
> 3. Margin equation:
>    $$\text{Winner} - \text{Loser} = 350$$[cite: 1]
>    $$50\%x - (40\%x - 150) = 350$$[cite: 1]
>    $$10\%x + 150 = 350 \implies 10\%x = 200 \implies x = 2000$$[cite: 1]
> 4. Calculations:
>    $$\text{Loser's Votes} = 40\%(2000) - 150 = 800 - 150 = 650$$[cite: 1]
>    $$\% \text{ of total registered} = \frac{650}{2000} \times 100 = \frac{65}{2}\% = 32.5\%$$[cite: 1]
> **Correct Option: (C)**[cite: 1]
