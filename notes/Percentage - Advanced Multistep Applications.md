> [!question]
> **Iterative Compound Stock Variation**
> At the beginning of Day 1 of a month, Rajesh has $500$ eggs[cite: 1]. He sells $20\%$ of the eggs by the end of the day and adds $b\%$ of the eggs at the beginning of the next day, then sells $20\%$ at the end of that day[cite: 1]. This pattern continues up to the end of the third day, when he is left with $1024$ eggs[cite: 1]. Find the value of $b$[cite: 1].
> - (A) $500$
> - (B) $20$
> - (C) $100$
> - (D) $10$[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> Selling $20\%$ leaves a factor of $0.80 = \frac{4}{5}$[cite: 1].
> Adding $b\%$ multiplies the quantity by $\left(1 + \frac{b}{100}\right)$[cite: 1].
> 1. **Day 1**:
>    - Start: $500$[cite: 1].
>    - End of Day 1: $500 \times 0.80 = 400$[cite: 1].
> 2. **Day 2**:
>    - Beginning of Day 2: $400 \left(1 + \frac{b}{100}\right)$[cite: 1].
>    - End of Day 2: $400 \left(1 + \frac{b}{100}\right) \times 0.80 = 320 \left(1 + \frac{b}{100}\right)$[cite: 1].
> 3. **Day 3**:
>    - Beginning of Day 3: $320 \left(1 + \frac{b}{100}\right) \times \left(1 + \frac{b}{100}\right) = 320 \left(1 + \frac{b}{100}\right)^2$.
>    - End of Day 3: $320 \left(1 + \frac{b}{100}\right)^2 \times 0.80 = 256 \left(1 + \frac{b}{100}\right)^2$[cite: 1].
> 4. Solve for $b$:
>    $$256 \left(1 + \frac{b}{100}\right)^2 = 1024$$[cite: 1]
>    $$\left(1 + \frac{b}{100}\right)^2 = \frac{1024}{256} = 4$$[cite: 1]
>    $$1 + \frac{b}{100} = 2 \implies \frac{b}{100} = 1 \implies b = 100$$[cite: 1]
> **Correct Option: (C) / 100**[cite: 1]

> [!question]
> **Fuel Consumption vs. Speed Variation (AFCAT 2 2025)**
> A truck travelling at $70\text{ km/h}$ uses $30\%$ more diesel to travel a certain distance than it does when travelling at $50\text{ km/h}$[cite: 1]. If the truck travels $19.5\text{ km}$ on a litre of diesel at $50\text{ km/h}$, how far can it travel on $10\text{ litres}$ of diesel at $70\text{ km/h}$?[cite: 1]
> - a) $130\text{ km}$
> - b) $140\text{ km}$
> - c) $150\text{ km}$
> - d) $175\text{ km}$[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> 1. At $50\text{ km/h}$, diesel consumption is $1\text{ L}$ for $19.5\text{ km}$[cite: 1].
> 2. At $70\text{ km/h}$, for that same distance ($19.5\text{ km}$), consumption is $30\%$ higher[cite: 1]:
>    $$\text{Diesel required} = 1 \times 1.30 = 1.3\text{ litres}$$[cite: 1]
> 3. Distance covered per litre at $70\text{ km/h}$:
>    $$\text{Efficiency} = \frac{19.5\text{ km}}{1.3\text{ L}} = 15\text{ km/L}$$[cite: 1]
> 4. Distance on $10\text{ litres}$ at $70\text{ km/h}$:
>    $$\text{Distance} = 15\text{ km/L} \times 10\text{ L} = 150\text{ km}$$[cite: 1]
> **Correct Option: c (150)**[cite: 1]

> **Weighted Sum Multiplier Shift (AFCAT 2 2025)**
> The ratio of weight of Ram and Shyam is $6 : 5$[cite: 1]. The weight of Ram increases by $15\%$[cite: 1]. The percentage increment in total weight is $20\%$ (an increase of $82.5\text{ kg}$)[cite: 1]. Find the percent change in the weight of Shyam[cite: 1].
> - 1. $20\%$
> - 2. $26\%$
> - 3. $30\%$
> - 4. None of these[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> Let initial weights be $R = 6x$ and $S = 5x$[cite: 1].
> Total initial weight $= 6x + 5x = 11x$[cite: 1].
> 1. Total increase $= 20\% \times 11x = 2.2x = \frac{11x \times 120}{100} - 11x = \frac{66x}{5} - 11x$[cite: 1].
> 2. Ram's increase $= 15\% \times 6x = \frac{15}{100} \times 6x = 0.9x = \frac{9x}{10}$[cite: 1].
> 3. Shyam's absolute increase:
>    $$\Delta S = \text{Total Increase} - \Delta R = 2.2x - 0.9x = 1.3x = \frac{13x}{10}$$[cite: 1]
> 4. Percentage change in Shyam's weight:
>    $$\% \Delta S = \frac{1.3x}{\text{Original } S} \times 100 = \frac{1.3x}{5x} \times 100 = \frac{130}{5} = 26\%$$[cite: 1]
> *(Note: The absolute increment value of $82.5\text{ kg}$ is superfluous since the question asks only for the percentage change)*[cite: 1].
> **Correct Option: 2 (26%)**[cite: 1]
