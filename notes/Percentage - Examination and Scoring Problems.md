> [!formula]
> Let $M$ be the total/max marks and $P$ be the passing marks[cite: 1].
> If candidate $A$ gets $a\%$ and fails by $f$ marks, while candidate $B$ gets $b\%$ and exceeds pass marks by $s$ marks[cite: 1]:
> $$P = M \cdot \left(\frac{a}{100}\right) + f = M \cdot \left(\frac{b}{100}\right) - s$$[cite: 1]
> $$M \cdot \left(\frac{b - a}{100}\right) = f + s \implies M = \frac{(f + s) \times 100}{b - a}$$[cite: 1]

> [!question]
> **Passing Standard Problem (Q10)**
> In a certain exam, $A$ got $38\%$ marks and failed by $18$ marks, whereas $B$ got $49\%$ marks and scored $15$ marks more than the passing marks[cite: 1]. Find the maximum marks of the exam[cite: 1].
> - 1. $300$
> - 2. $650$
> - 3. $778$
> - 4. $256$[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> Difference in percentages:
> $$49\% - 38\% = 11\% \text{ of } M$$[cite: 1]
> Difference in scores:
> $$18\text{ (deficit)} + 15\text{ (surplus)} = 33\text{ marks}$$[cite: 1]
> Equating:
> $$11\% \text{ of } M = 33 \implies M = \frac{33}{11} \times 100 = 300$$[cite: 1]
> **Correct Option: 1**[cite: 1]

> [!question]
> **Homework Exam Problem (AFCAT 1 2024)**
> A student scores $40\%$ marks and fails by $40$ marks in the examination, and another student scores $60\%$ and scores $20$ marks more than pass marks[cite: 1]. Find the maximum marks for the examination[cite: 1].
> - 1. $100$
> - 2. $200$
> - 3. $300$
> - 4. $400$[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> Difference in percentage $= 60\% - 40\% = 20\%$[cite: 1].
> Difference in marks $= 40 + 20 = 60$ marks[cite: 1].
> $$20\% \text{ of } M = 60 \implies M = \frac{60}{20} \times 100 = 300$$[cite: 1]
> **Correct Option: 3**[cite: 1]

> [!question]
> **Simultaneous Equations in Examination (Olympiad Problem)**
> In an Olympiad exam, $20\%$ of boys and $15\%$ of girls failed[cite: 1]. The number of boys who passed was $70$ more than that of the girls who passed[cite: 1]. A total of $90$ students failed[cite: 1]. Find the total number of students who appeared for the exam[cite: 1].
> - (A) $500$
> - (B) $350$
> - (C) $400$
> - (D) $420$[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> Let total boys be $B$ and total girls be $G$.
> 1. Failure equation:
>    $$\frac{20}{100}B + \frac{15}{100}G = 90 \implies \frac{1}{5}B + \frac{3}{20}G = 90$$[cite: 1]
>    Multiply by $20$:
>    $$4B + 3G = 1800 \quad \text{--- (1)}$$[cite: 1]
> 2. Pass relation:
>    $$\text{Passed boys} = 80\% B = \frac{4}{5}B = \frac{16}{20}B$$[cite: 1]
>    $$\text{Passed girls} = 85\% G = \frac{17}{20}G$$[cite: 1]
>    $$\frac{16}{20}B - \frac{17}{20}G = 70 \implies 16B - 17G = 1400 \quad \text{--- (2)}$$[cite: 1]
> 3. Multiply equation (1) by $4$:
>    $$16B + 12G = 7200 \quad \text{--- (3)}$$[cite: 1]
>    Subtract equation (2) from (3):
>    $$29G = 5800 \implies G = 200$$[cite: 1]
> 4. Substitute $G = 200$ into equation (1):
>    $$4B + 3(200) = 1800 \implies 4B = 1200 \implies B = 300$$[cite: 1]
> 5. Total appeared:
>    $$\text{Total} = B + G = 300 + 200 = 500$$[cite: 1]
> **Correct Option: (A)**[cite: 1]

> [!question]
> **Linear Ratio Relation (CDS 2 2025)**
> Two students $X$ and $Y$ appeared in a test[cite: 1]. The score of $X$ is $20$ more than that of $Y$[cite: 1]. If the score of $X$ is $75\%$ of the sum of the scores of $X$ and $Y$, what is the ratio of the score of $X$ to the score of $Y$?[cite: 1]
> - (a) $5 : 1$
> - (b) $4 : 1$
> - (c) $3 : 1$
> - (d) $3 : 2$[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> Let score of $Y = a \implies$ score of $X = a + 20$[cite: 1].
> Sum of scores $= X + Y = 2a + 20$[cite: 1].
> Given:
> $$a + 20 = 75\%(2a + 20) = \frac{3}{4}(2a + 20)$$[cite: 1]
> $$4(a + 20) = 3(2a + 20) \implies 4a + 80 = 6a + 60$$[cite: 1]
> $$2a = 20 \implies a = 10$$[cite: 1]
> Scores: $Y = 10$, $X = 10 + 20 = 30$[cite: 1].
> Ratio $X : Y$:
> $$\frac{X}{Y} = \frac{30}{10} = \frac{3}{1} = 3 : 1$$[cite: 1]
> **Correct Option: (c)**[cite: 1]
