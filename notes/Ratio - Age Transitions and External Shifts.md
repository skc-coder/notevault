> [!theorem]
> **The Constant Difference Invariant in Age Problems**
> The chronological difference between two individuals' ages remains identical across all points in time:
> $$A(t_2) - B(t_2) = A(t_1) - B(t_1) = \Delta K \quad (\forall t_1, t_2)$$
> When using ratio units to model aging, balance both ratios so that their unit difference $(b_1 - a_1) = (b_2 - a_2)$ is equal[cite: 1]. Once balanced, unit shifts match elapsed years directly[cite: 1].

> [!question]
> **Type 11: Two-Point Chronological Shift**
> $5\text{ years ago}$, the ratio of ages of $X$ and $Y$ was $2 : 3$[cite: 1]. After $5\text{ years}$, the ratio will be $3 : 4$[cite: 1]. Find the present age of $X$[cite: 1].
> - 1. $20$
> - 2. $35$
> - 3. $30$
> - 4. $25$[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> - **Method 1 (Ratio Difference Invariant)**:
>   - $5\text{ years ago}$: $X : Y = 2 : 3$ (Difference $= 3 - 2 = 1\text{ unit}$)[cite: 1]
>   - $5\text{ years hence}$: $X : Y = 3 : 4$ (Difference $= 4 - 3 = 1\text{ unit}$)[cite: 1]
>   The differences are already balanced ($1\text{ unit}$)[cite: 1].
>   Unit change for $X$:
>   $$\Delta = 3 - 2 = 1\text{ unit}$$[cite: 1]
>   Elapsed chronological time:
>   $$\Delta t = 5\text{ years ago to } 5\text{ years hence} = 5 + 5 = 10\text{ years}$$[cite: 1]
>   $$1\text{ unit} = 10\text{ years}$$[cite: 1]
>   Age of $X$ $5\text{ years ago}$:
>   $$X_{-5} = 2\text{ units} = 2 \times 10 = 20\text{ years}$$[cite: 1]
>   Present age:
>   $$X_{\text{present}} = 20 + 5 = 25\text{ years}$$[cite: 1]
> - **Method 2 (Algebraic Formulation)**:
>   $$\frac{x - 5}{y - 5} = \frac{2}{3} \implies 3x - 2y = 5$$[cite: 1]
>   $$\frac{x + 5}{y + 5} = \frac{3}{4} \implies 4x - 3y = -5$$[cite: 1]
>   Solving simultaneously yields $x = 25$[cite: 1].
> **Correct Option: 4 (25)**[cite: 1]

> [!question]
> **Type 12: Uniform Scalar Subtraction**
> Two numbers are in the ratio $3 : 5$[cite: 1]. If $9$ is subtracted from both numbers, the ratio becomes $12 : 23$[cite: 1]. Find the original numbers[cite: 1].
> - 1. $22, 55$
> - 2. $33, 55$
> - 3. $44, 11$
> - 4. None[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> Let the numbers be $3x$ and $5x$[cite: 1].
> Formulate the subtraction equation:
> $$\frac{3x - 9}{5x - 9} = \frac{12}{23}$$[cite: 1]
> Cross-multiply:
> $$23(3x - 9) = 12(5x - 9)$$[cite: 1]
> $$69x - 207 = 60x - 108$$[cite: 1]
> $$9x = 99 \implies x = 11$$[cite: 1]
> Compute the numbers:
> - First number $= 3x = 3(11) = 33$[cite: 1]
> - Second number $= 5x = 5(11) = 55$[cite: 1]
> **Correct Option: 2 (33, 55)**[cite: 1]

> [!question]
> **PYQ (AFCAT 2 2025): Fractional Multiplier Reversal**
> The numerator of a fraction is decreased by $25\%$ and its denominator is increased by $220\%$[cite: 1]. If the resulting fraction is $\frac{3}{2}$, find the original fraction[cite: 1].
> - 1. $32/5$
> - 2. $22/5$
> - 3. $11/5$
> - 4. None[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> Let the original fraction be $\frac{x}{y}$[cite: 1].
> Apply multipliers:
> - Numerator becomes $x \times (1 - 0.25) = 0.75x = \frac{75}{100}x$[cite: 1]
> - Denominator becomes $y \times (1 + 2.20) = 3.20y = \frac{320}{100}y$[cite: 1]
> Equating to the final value:
> $$\frac{\frac{75}{100}x}{\frac{320}{100}y} = \frac{3}{2} \implies \frac{75x}{320y} = \frac{3}{2}$$[cite: 1]
> Solve for $\frac{x}{y}$:
> $$\frac{x}{y} = \frac{3}{2} \times \frac{320}{75}$$[cite: 1]
> Cancel common factors:
> $$\frac{3}{75} = \frac{1}{25} \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{320}{2} = 160$$[cite: 1]
> $$\frac{x}{y} = \frac{160}{25} = \frac{32}{5}$$[cite: 1]
> **Correct Option: 1**[cite: 1]
