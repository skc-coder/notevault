> [!definition]
> A **Regular Language** is a language recognized by a Finite Automaton (FA / DFA / NFA) or generated by a Regular Expression (RE) / Regular Grammar[cite: 1]. It possesses strictly finite memory (state-bounded) and cannot perform unbounded counting or cross-referencing between disjoint substrings[cite: 1].

> [!theorem]
> ### The Single-Symbol & AP Criterion
> 1. **Over a Single-Symbol Alphabet ($|\Sigma| = 1$):** https://tinyurl.com/2w4bknfe
>    An infinite language over $\Sigma = \{a\}$ is regular **if and only if** the lengths of its valid strings form an **Arithmetic Progression (AP)** or a union of APs[cite: 1]:
>    $$\text{Lengths} = \{k \cdot p + c \mid k \ge 0\} \iff \text{Regular}$$
>    - $L = \{a^{2n} \mid n \ge 0\} = (aa)^* \implies$ Common difference $d = 2$ (AP) $\implies$ **Regular**[cite: 1].
>    - $L = \{a^p \mid p \text{ is prime}\} = \{a^2, a^3, a^5, a^7, a^{11}, \dots\} \implies$ Step differences $\{1, 2, 2, 4, 2, \dots\}$ are non-constant $\implies$ **Non-Regular**[cite: 1].
>    - $L = \{a^{n^2} \mid n \ge 0\}, L = \{a^{2^n}\}, L = \{a^{n!}\} \implies$ Differences grow without bound $\implies$ **Non-Regular**[cite: 1].
> 2. **Finite Languages are Always Regular:**
>    Every finite subset of $\Sigma^*$ is strictly regular because it can be modeled as a finite tree of states[cite: 1].
>    - $L = \{a^p \mid p \text{ is prime}, p < 100\} \implies$ Finite $\implies$ **Regular**[cite: 1].
> 3. **Unbounded Dependency / Memory Traps:**
>    - $L = \{0^n 1^n \mid n \ge 0\} \implies$ Requires infinite counting memory to equate zeros and ones $\implies$ **Non-Regular** (requires stack $\implies$ CFL)[cite: 1].
>    - $L = \{w w \mid w \in \{0, 1\}^*\} \implies$ Requires remembering an arbitrary-length prefix string $\implies$ **Non-Regular** (requires 2-way read/write tape $\implies$ CSL)[cite: 1].
>    - $L = \{0^n 1^n 0^n \mid n \ge 0\} \implies$ 3-way synchronization $\implies$ **Non-Regular & Not CFL**[cite: 1].

> [!formula]
> ### Quick Identification Shortcuts for Exam Hall Speed
> | Language Form | Condition | Class | Justification |
> | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
> | $a^m b^n$ | $m, n \ge 0$ (independent) | Regular | RE $= a^* b^*$[cite: 1] |
> | $a^m b^n$ | $m = \text{even}, n = \text{odd}$ | Regular | Modulo-2 arithmetic trackable by finite states[cite: 1] |
> | $a^m b^n$ | $m < n < 10$ | Regular | Finite set[cite: 1] |
> | $a^m b^n$ | $m = n, m > 10$ | Non-Regular | Unbounded memory required for $m > 10$[cite: 1] |
> | $a^m b^n$ | $\gcd(m, n) = 1$ | Non-Regular | Unbounded relational comparison[cite: 1] |
> | $a^m b^n$ | $\text{LCM}(m, n) = 1$ | Regular | Only holds for $m=1, n=1 \implies \{ab\}$, finite[cite: 1] |
> | $w x w^R$ or $w x w$ | $w \in \{a, b\}^*, x \in \{a, b\}^+$ | Regular | Can collapse $w = \epsilon \implies (a+b)^+$[cite: 1] |

> [!trap]
> **The Independent Star vs Dependent Power Trap:**
> Do not confuse $a^* b^*$ with $a^n b^n$[cite: 1]. Star operations on separate symbols operate completely independently ($m$ and $n$ vary without constraint)[cite: 1]. A shared variable index ($n$) enforces a strict lockstep equality constraint that destroys finite automaton tractability unless bounded by a constant[cite: 1].

> [!question]
> **IOCL CBT Practice Question:**
> Which of the following languages over $\Sigma = \{0, 1\}$ is accepted by a Finite Automaton?
> (A) $L_1 = \{0^n 1^n \mid n \ge 0\}$
> (B) $L_2 = \{w w^R \mid w \in \{0, 1\}^*\}$
> (C) $L_3 = \{w \mid w \text{ contains an even number of } 0\text{'s}\}$
> (D) $L_4 = \{0^n 1^n 0^n \mid n \ge 0\}$
>
> **Answer:** (C)
> **Step-by-Step Explanation:**
> 1. $L_1$ requires a counter to match the number of zeros and ones $\implies$ infinite memory needed $\implies$ Context-Free, not Regular[cite: 1].
> 2. $L_2$ is the set of even-length palindromes, requiring a LIFO stack to verify reverse order $\implies$ Non-deterministic CFL, not Regular[cite: 1].
> 3. $L_3$ only monitors whether the running count of $0$'s modulo 2 is 0[cite: 1]. This needs only two states: $q_{\text{even}}$ and $q_{\text{odd}}$[cite: 1]. Because $1$'s are unconstrained, they form self-loops[cite: 1]. Hence, $L_3$ is strictly Regular[cite: 1].
> 4. $L_4$ requires simultaneous multi-symbol tracking $\implies$ Context-Sensitive Language (CSL)[cite: 1].
