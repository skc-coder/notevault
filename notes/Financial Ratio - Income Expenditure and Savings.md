> [!formula]
> **The Accounting Balance Identity**:
> $$\text{Income } (I) = \text{Expenditure } (E) + \text{Savings } (S)$$[cite: 1]
> $$\implies E = I - S \quad \text{and} \quad S = I - E$$[cite: 1]
> 
> **Cross-Multiplication Method for Simultaneous Savings**:
> Given income ratio $I_1 : I_2 = a : b$ and expenditure ratio $E_1 : E_2 = c : d$, with savings $S_1$ and $S_2$[cite: 1]:
> $$\frac{ax - S_1}{bx - S_2} = \frac{c}{d} \implies d(ax - S_1) = c(bx - S_2)$$[cite: 1]
> Solving for scale factor $x$:
> $$x = \frac{|d \cdot S_1 - c \cdot S_2|}{|a \cdot d - b \cdot c|}$$[cite: 1]

> [!question]
> **Type 8: Multi-Person Income-Expenditure Bridge**
> The incomes of $A$ and $B$ are in the ratio $5 : 3$[cite: 1]. The expenses of $A, B,$ and $C$ are in the ratio $8 : 5 : 2$[cite: 1]. If $C$ spends Rs. $2000$ and $B$ saves Rs. $700$, find $A$'s savings[cite: 1].
> - 1. $1500$
> - 2. $4000$
> - 3. $3590$
> - 4. $1400$[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> Let incomes be $I_A = 5y, I_B = 3y$[cite: 1].
> Let expenditures be $E_A = 8x, E_B = 5x, E_C = 2x$[cite: 1].
> 1. Determine expenditure scale from $C$:
>    $$2x = 2000 \implies x = 1000$$[cite: 1]
>    - $E_A = 8(1000) = \text{Rs. } 8000$[cite: 1]
>    - $E_B = 5(1000) = \text{Rs. } 5000$[cite: 1]
> 2. Determine income scale using $B$'s savings:
>    $$I_B - E_B = S_B \implies 3y - 5000 = 700 \implies 3y = 5700 \implies y = 1900$$[cite: 1]
> 3. Calculate $A$'s income and savings:
>    $$I_A = 5y = 5(1900) = \text{Rs. } 9500$$[cite: 1]
>    $$\text{Savings}_A = I_A - E_A = 9500 - 8000 = \text{Rs. } 1500$$[cite: 1]
> **Correct Option: 1**[cite: 1]

> **Type 9: Dual Income-Expenditure System**
> The ratio of incomes of two persons is $5 : 3$ and that of their expenditures is $9 : 5$[cite: 1]. If they save Rs. $2600$ and Rs. $1800$ respectively, find their incomes[cite: 1].
> - 1. $8000\text{ and } 4800$
> - 2. $6000\text{ and } 6000$
> - 3. $10,000\text{ and } 500$
> - 4. $9000\text{ and } 5400$[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> Set up simultaneous equations using income multiplier $x$ and expenditure multiplier $y$[cite: 1]:
> 1. $5x - 9y = 2600$[cite: 1]
> 2. $3x - 5y = 1800$[cite: 1]
> Eliminate $y$:
> Multiply equation (1) by $5 \implies 25x - 45y = 13000$[cite: 1]
> Multiply equation (2) by $9 \implies 27x - 45y = 16200$[cite: 1]
> Subtracting equation (1) from (2):
> $$2x = 3200 \implies x = 1600$$[cite: 1]
> Compute incomes:
> - $\text{Income}_A = 5x = 5(1600) = \text{Rs. } 8000$[cite: 1]
> - $\text{Income}_B = 3x = 3(1600) = \text{Rs. } 4800$[cite: 1]
> **Correct Option: 1**[cite: 1]

> [!question]
> **Fractional Income-to-Savings Constraint**
> The incomes of $A, B,$ and $C$ are in the ratio $7 : 9 : 12$ and their expenditures are in the ratio $8 : 9 : 15$[cite: 1]. If $A$'s saving is $\frac{1}{4}$ of his income, find the ratio of savings of $A, B,$ and $C$[cite: 1].
> - 1. $56 : 99 : 69$
> - 2. $99 : 56 : 69$
> - 3. $69 : 56 : 99$
> - 4. $99 : 69 : 56$[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> Let incomes be $7x, 9x, 12x$ and expenditures be $8y, 9y, 15y$[cite: 1].
> 1. Relate multipliers using $A$'s savings:
>    $$\text{Savings}_A = 7x - 8y = \frac{7x}{4}$$[cite: 1]
>    $$7x - \frac{7x}{4} = 8y \implies \frac{21x}{4} = 8y \implies 21x = 32y \implies \frac{x}{y} = \frac{32}{21}$$[cite: 1]
> 2. Substitute common parameter $k$ ($x = 32k, y = 21k$)[cite: 1]:
>    - Incomes:
>      - $I_A = 7(32k) = 224k$[cite: 1]
>      - $I_B = 9(32k) = 288k$[cite: 1]
>      - $I_C = 12(32k) = 384k$[cite: 1]
>    - Expenditures:
>      - $E_A = 8(21k) = 168k$[cite: 1]
>      - $E_B = 9(21k) = 189k$[cite: 1]
>      - $E_C = 15(21k) = 315k$[cite: 1]
> 3. Calculate savings ($I - E$):
>    - $S_A = 224k - 168k = 56k$[cite: 1]
>    - $S_B = 288k - 189k = 99k$[cite: 1]
>    - $S_C = 384k - 315k = 69k$[cite: 1]
>    $$S_A : S_B : S_C = 56 : 99 : 69$$[cite: 1]
> **Correct Option: 1**[cite: 1]

> **PYQ (AFCAT 1 2025): Remainder Expenditure Cascade**
> A person spends $\frac{1}{3}$ of income on rent, $\frac{1}{4}$ on food, and $\frac{1}{6}$ on clothes[cite: 1]. Of the remainder left, $\frac{1}{10}$ equals Rs. $13,500$[cite: 1]. Find the difference between the shares spent on food and clothes[cite: 1].
> - 1. $30,000$
> - 2. $45,000$
> - 3. $33,000$
> - 4. None[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> Let total income be $x$[cite: 1].
> 1. Sum fractional expenditures using common denominator $12$[cite: 1]:
>    $$\text{Spent} = \frac{x}{3} + \frac{x}{4} + \frac{x}{6} = \frac{4x + 3x + 2x}{12} = \frac{9x}{12} = \frac{3x}{4}$$[cite: 1]
>    $$\text{Remainder} = x - \frac{3x}{4} = \frac{x}{4}$$[cite: 1]
> 2. Apply the given residual condition:
>    $$\frac{1}{10} \times \left(\frac{x}{4}\right) = 13500 \implies \frac{x}{40} = 13500 \implies x = 540,000$$[cite: 1]
> 3. Compute difference between food and clothes:
>    $$\text{Food} - \text{Clothes} = \frac{x}{4} - \frac{x}{6} = \frac{3x - 2x}{12} = \frac{x}{12}$$[cite: 1]
>    $$\text{Difference} = \frac{540000}{12} = \text{Rs. } 45,000$$[cite: 1]
> **Correct Option: 2**[cite: 1]

> **PYQ (AFCAT 1 2024): Scaled Percentage Increments**
> The salaries of $A, B,$ and $C$ are in the ratio $5 : 3 : 2$[cite: 1]. If increments of $20\%, 25\%,$ and $30\%$ respectively are added, what will be the new ratio of salaries?[cite: 1]
> - 1. $122 : 11 : 39$
> - 2. $122 : 10 : 8$
> - 3. $120 : 75 : 52$
> - 4. $120 : 71 : 59$[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> Multiply baseline ratios by their respective percentage growth multipliers ($1 + \Delta\%$)[cite: 1]:
> - $A_{\text{new}} = 5 \times 1.20 = 5 \times \frac{120}{100} = 6.0 = \frac{600}{100}$[cite: 1]
> - $B_{\text{new}} = 3 \times 1.25 = 3 \times \frac{125}{100} = 3.75 = \frac{375}{100}$[cite: 1]
> - $C_{\text{new}} = 2 \times 1.30 = 2 \times \frac{130}{100} = 2.60 = \frac{260}{100}$[cite: 1]
> Multiply through by $100$ to clear decimals:
> $$600 : 375 : 260$$[cite: 1]
> Divide by common factor $5$:
> $$\frac{600}{5} : \frac{375}{5} : \frac{260}{5} = 120 : 75 : 52$$[cite: 1]
> **Correct Option: 3**[cite: 1]

> [!question]
> **PYQ (CDS 2 2024): Data Sufficiency in Salary Systems**
> **Question**: The ratio of $P$'s salary to $Q$'s salary is $6 : 5$[cite: 1]. How much is $P$'s expenditure?[cite: 1]
> - **Statement-I**: The ratio of $P$'s saving to $Q$'s saving is $3 : 2$[cite: 1].
> - **Statement-II**: The ratio of $P$'s expenditure to $Q$'s expenditure is $1 : 1$[cite: 1].
> 
> *Options:*
> - (a) Statement I alone is sufficient[cite: 1].
> - (b) Either Statement alone is sufficient[cite: 1].
> - (c) Both Statements together are sufficient[cite: 1].
> - (d) The Question cannot be answered even using both Statements together[cite: 1].
> 
> *Solution:*
> Let salaries be $I_P = 6x, I_Q = 5x$[cite: 1].
> - Statement-I provides $S_P : S_Q = 3 : 2 \implies$ ratio only, no absolute value[cite: 1].
> - Statement-II provides $E_P : E_Q = 1 : 1 \implies$ ratio only, no absolute value[cite: 1].
> Combining both statements:
> $$6x - E = 3y \quad \text{and} \quad 5x - E = 2y$$[cite: 1]
> Subtracting the equations gives $x = y$, which yields $E = 3x$ (or savings $S = 3x$)[cite: 1].
> Because every equation remains homogeneous, the scale factor $x$ cannot be determined without at least one absolute numerical anchor (e.g., total salary in rupees)[cite: 1].
> The absolute value of $P$'s expenditure cannot be determined[cite: 1].
> **Correct Option: (d)**[cite: 1]

> **PYQ (CDS 2 2025): Opposing Successive Adjustments**
> There are two employees $X$ and $Y$[cite: 1]. $X$'s salary is increased by $12\%$ and then decreased by $10\%$[cite: 1]. $Y$'s salary is increased by $10\%$ and then decreased by $12\%$[cite: 1]. If their final salaries are equal, find the ratio of the initial salary of $X$ to that of $Y$[cite: 1].
> - (a) $50 : 53$
> - (b) $51 : 53$
> - (c) $121 : 126$
> - (d) $121 : 125$[cite: 1]
> 
> *Solution:*
> Apply multipliers to each initial salary[cite: 1]:
> $$X \times \left(\frac{112}{100}\right) \times \left(\frac{90}{100}\right) = Y \times \left(\frac{110}{100}\right) \times \left(\frac{88}{100}\right)$$[cite: 1]
> Clear common factors:
> $$X \times (112 \times 90) = Y \times (110 \times 88)$$[cite: 1]
> Divide both sides by $10$:
> $$X \times (112 \times 9) = Y \times (11 \times 88)$$[cite: 1]
> Divide $112$ and $88$ by $8$:
> $$X \times (14 \times 9) = Y \times (11 \times 11)$$[cite: 1]
> $$X \times 126 = Y \times 121$$[cite: 1]
> Ratio of initial salaries:
> $$\frac{X}{Y} = \frac{121}{126} \implies X : Y = 121 : 126$$[cite: 1]
> **Correct Option: (c)**[cite: 1]
