## 1. Hardness of Water & Treatment Methods

Hard water does not produce lather easily with soap and forms scum. Caused by dissolved Bicarbonates, Chlorides, and Sulphates of Calcium ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$) and Magnesium ($\text{Mg}^{2+}$).

| Type of Hardness | Causing Salts | Treatment / Removal Method | Memory Hook / Exam Shortcut |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Temporary Hardness** | Calcium Bicarbonate $\text{Ca(HCO}_3)_2$<br>Magnesium Bicarbonate $\text{Mg(HCO}_3)_2$ | 1. **Boiling:** Converts soluble bicarbonates to insoluble carbonates ($\text{Ca(HCO}_3)_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{CaCO}_3\downarrow + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2\uparrow$).<br>2. **Clark's Method:** Adding calculated amount of Slaked Lime $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$. | **"Temp = Bicarbonate = Boiling"**<br>Easily removed just by boiling or adding lime. |
| **Permanent Hardness** | Calcium Chloride $\text{CaCl}_2$, $\text{CaSO}_4$<br>Magnesium Chloride $\text{MgCl}_2$, $\text{MgSO}_4$ | 1. **Washing Soda Method:** Treatment with $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ ($\text{CaCl}_2 + \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 \rightarrow \text{CaCO}_3\downarrow + 2\text{NaCl}$).<br>2. **Calgon Process:** Using Sodium Hexametaphosphate ($\text{Na}_6\text{P}_6\text{O}_{18}$).<br>3. **Ion-Exchange Resin Method:** Cation and Anion exchange resins (yields pure demineralized water). | **"Perm = Chloride & Sulphate"**<br>Boiling FAILS! Requires chemicals (Washing Soda, Calgon, or Resins). |

> [!TIP]
> **Hard Water vs Soft Water Quick Summary:**
> * **Soft Water:** Lathers easily with soap; free from $\text{Ca}^{2+} / \text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions. (e.g., Rainwater, distilled water).
> * **Hard Water:** Does NOT lather easily; forms sticky **scum** ($\text{Ca-soap}$ ppt). Soap is wasted! Synthetic detergents work in both hard and soft water.

* **Heavy Water ($\text{D}_2\text{O}$):** Deuterium Oxide. Used as a **Moderator** in nuclear reactors to slow down fast neutrons. (Discovered by Harold Urey).

## 2. Industrial Materials: Glass, Cement, and Ceramics

### A. Glass (Supercooled Liquid)
Glass is an amorphous, non-crystalline inorganic solid. It is considered a **supercooled liquid** because it does not have a sharp melting point and flows slowly over years.

* **Raw Materials:** Silica / Sand ($\text{SiO}_2$), Soda Ash ($\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$), Limestone ($\text{CaCO}_3$).
* **Annealing:** Process of slow, controlled cooling of hot glass to eliminate internal stress and prevent brittleness.
* **Types of Glass & Uses:**
  * **Pyrex / Borosilicate Glass:** Contains Boron Oxide ($\text{B}_2\text{O}_3$); high thermal shock resistance. Used for laboratory glassware and ovenware.
  * **Flint Glass:** Contains Lead Oxide ($\text{PbO}$); high refractive index. Used for optical lenses, prisms, and premium glassware.
  * **Soda-Lime Glass:** Common window glass and bottles.
  * **Crookes Glass:** Contains Cerium Oxide ($\text{CeO}_2$); **absorbs harmful UV rays**. Used in sunglasses and protective spectacles.
* **Glass Coloring Agents:**
  * Cobalt Oxide ($\text{CoO}$) $\rightarrow$ **Deep Blue** | Cuprous Oxide ($\text{Cu}_2\text{O}$) $\rightarrow$ **Red**
  * Chromium Oxide ($\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_3$) $\rightarrow$ **Green** | Ferric Oxide ($\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$) $\rightarrow$ **Yellow / Brown**

### B. Portland Cement (Joseph Aspdin, 1824)
Fine grey powder manufactured by calcining a mixture of Limestone ($\text{CaCO}_3$) and Clay at $1400-1500^\circ\text{C}$ to form **Clinker**, which is ground with Gypsum.

* **Essential Chemical Composition:**
  1. Lime ($\text{CaO}$): $60 - 67\%$ (Provides strength)
  2. Silica ($\text{SiO}_2$): $17 - 25\%$ (Provides strength)
  3. Alumina ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$): $3 - 8\%$ (Quick setting property)
  4. Iron Oxide ($\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$): $0.5 - 6\%$ (Provides color and hardness)
* **Role of Gypsum ($\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$):** Added ($2 - 3\%$) during final grinding of clinker to **slow down the initial setting time of cement** (prevents quick setting when mixed with water).

## 3. Chemical Fertilizers (N-P-K)

Fertilizers supply essential nutrients (Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium) to soil.

1. **Nitrogenous Fertilizers:**
   * **Urea ($\text{NH}_2\text{CONH}_2$):** Contains **$46\%$ Nitrogen** (highest N-content solid fertilizer). Organic compound synthesized in industry from $\text{NH}_3$ and $\text{CO}_2$.
   * **Ammonium Nitrate ($\text{NH}_4\text{NO}_3$):** Source of nitrogen; highly explosive if contaminated.
   * **Calcium Ammonium Nitrate (CAN):** Known as *Kisan Khad*; neutral fertilizer.
2. **Phosphatic Fertilizers:**
   * **Single Superphosphate (SSP):** Manufactured from Rock Phosphate and Sulphuric Acid. Contains $\text{Ca(H}_2\text{PO}_4)_2$ and Gypsum.
3. **Potassic Fertilizers:**
   * Muriate of Potash / Potassium Chloride ($\text{KCl}$).

## 4. Explosives & Pyrotechnics

* **Gunpowder (Black Powder):** Mixture of **Potassium Nitrate ($\text{KNO}_3 \sim 75\%$, Saltpetre)** + **Charcoal ($\text{C} \sim 15\%$)** + **Sulphur ($\text{S} \sim 10\%$)**.
  * **Role of Sulphur in Gunpowder:** Acts as a fuel and **lowers the ignition temperature** to accelerate combustion speed.
* **Dynamite (Alfred Nobel, 1867):** Originally made by absorbing liquid **Nitroglycerin** into inert porous diatomaceous earth (Kieselguhr) to stabilize it.
* **TNT (Trinitrotoluene):** $\text{C}_6\text{H}_2(\text{NO}_2)_3\text{CH}_3$. High explosive produced by nitration of toluene.
* **RDX (Research Department Explosive):** Cyclotrimethylenetrinitramine ($\text{C}_3\text{H}_6\text{N}_6\text{O}_6$). Also known as *Cyclonite* or *Hexogen*.
* **Airbag Chemistry in Automobiles:** Airbags deploy upon collision impact triggered by rapid thermal decomposition of **Sodium Azide ($\text{NaN}_3$)** to generate massive volume of **Nitrogen Gas ($\text{N}_2\uparrow$)**:
  $$2\text{NaN}_3\text{(s)} \xrightarrow{\text{Impact Spark}} 2\text{Na(s)} + 3\text{N}_2\text{(g)}\uparrow$$

## 5. Paints, Polymers & Binders

* **Paints Components:**
  1. **Pigment:** Provides color and opacity (e.g., Titanium Dioxide $\text{TiO}_2$ white, Zinc oxide).
  2. **Binder / Resin:** Forms a continuous dry film holding pigment particles together (e.g., Linseed oil, Alkyd resins, Polyurethane, Acrylics).
  3. **Solvent / Thinner:** Adjusts viscosity for spraying/brushing (e.g., Turpentine, Mineral spirits).
* **Silicones:** Synthetic organosilicon polymers with repeating $-\text{Si-O-Si}-$ backbone. Water-repellent, heat-resistant, used as sealants, lubricants, and synthetic rubber.

## 6. Solved PYQs (NDA / CDS / CAPF)

### Q1 (NDA II 2024)
**Question:** Which one among the following fertilizers is a rich source of Nitrogen?
*(a) Urea | (b) Single Superphosphate | (c) Muriate of Potash | (d) NPK 10:26:26*
* **Answer:** **(a) Urea** ($46\%\text{ N}$).

### Q2 (NDA I 2019)
**Question:** Which one of the following is used as a binder in paints?
*(a) Silicones | (b) Titanium dioxide | (c) Polyurethane / Resin | (d) Turpentine*
* **Answer:** **(c) Polyurethane / Resin / Linseed oil**.

### Q3 (NDA II 2013)
**Question:** What is the presence of sulphur in gunpowder for?
*(a) To act as an oxidizing agent | (b) To lower ignition temperature and speed up burning | (c) To absorb moisture | (d) To increase weight*
* **Answer:** **(b) To lower ignition temperature and speed up burning**.

### Q4 (CAPF 2020)
**Question:** Airbags in cars work on the principle of a chemical reaction triggered by impact producing which gas?
*(a) Oxygen | (b) Nitrogen | (c) Hydrogen | (d) Carbon monoxide*
* **Answer:** **(b) Nitrogen ($\text{N}_2$)** from Sodium Azide ($\text{NaN}_3$).
