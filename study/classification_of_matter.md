# Classification of Matter & Physical States

Exhaustive chapter notes covering the Pancha Tattva, 5 States of Matter (Solid, Liquid, Gas, Plasma, Bose-Einstein Condensate), Kinetic Molecular Theory, Gas Laws, Latent Heat, True Solutions, Colloids, Suspensions, Separation Techniques, Water Treatment, and Physical vs Chemical Changes.

---

## 1. Early Philosophical Concepts & Definition of Matter

* **Pancha Tattva (Ancient Philosophy):** Ancient Indian philosophers (Maharishi Kanad) and early Greeks classified matter into 5 basic elements: **Air, Earth, Fire, Sky, and Water**.
* **Modern Definition of Matter:** Anything that has mass and occupies volume/space.
* **Particle Nature of Matter:**
  1. Matter is made of extremely small particles (atoms / molecules).
  2. Particles have spaces between them (*intermolecular spaces*).
  3. Particles are in continuous motion (*kinetic energy increases with temperature*: $KE \propto T$).
  4. Particles exert forces of attraction on each other (*intermolecular force of attraction*: Solids > Liquids > Gases).
* **Diffusion:** Intermixing of particles of two different substances on their own. Rate of diffusion increases with heating ($T\uparrow \implies \text{Diffusion rate}\uparrow$).

## 2. The 5 States of Matter

```
                   +-----------------------+
                   |  PLASMA (4th State)   | (Super-energetic, Ionized Gas)
                   +-----------------------+
                              ^
                    Heating   |  Ionization
                              v
[ SOLID ] ===(Melting)===> [ LIQUID ] ===(Boiling)===> [ GAS ]
   ^                          |                          |
   |                          +---(Sublimation)----------+
   |
   +---(Super-cooling low density gas near 0 K)---> [ BOSE-EINSTEIN CONDENSATE (5th State) ]
```

### A. Solid, Liquid, and Gas Comparison

| Property | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Shape & Volume** | Fixed shape & fixed volume | No fixed shape (takes container shape); Fixed volume | No fixed shape & no fixed volume |
| **Compressibility** | Negligible | Low / Rigid | High (e.g., LPG, CNG) |
| **Intermolecular Space** | Minimum (closely packed) | Intermediate | Maximum (loosely packed) |
| **Intermolecular Force** | Maximum | Intermediate | Minimum / Negligible |
| **Kinetic Energy** | Minimum | Intermediate | Maximum |
| **Flow / Rigidity** | Rigid (does not flow) | Fluid (can flow) | Highly Fluid |

### B. The 4th & 5th States of Matter

1. **Plasma (4th State):**
   * **Nature:** Super-energetic and super-excited particles in the form of **ionized gas**.
   * **Occurrence:** Neon sign bulbs (neon gas) and fluorescent tubes (helium/gas). When electric energy flows, the gas gets ionized and glows.
   * **Sun & Stars:** Stars glow because of plasma formed due to extremely high temperatures and thermonuclear fusion.
2. **Bose-Einstein Condensate (5th State):**
   * **Prediction:** Predicted in 1920 by Indian physicist **Satyendra Nath Bose** and **Albert Einstein**.
   * **Realization:** Formed by super-cooling a gas of extremely low density (about $1/100,000\text{th}$ the density of normal air) to super-low temperatures near Absolute Zero ($0\text{ K}$ or $-273.15^\circ\text{C}$).
   * **Nobel Prize (2001):** Eric Cornell, Carl Wieman, and Wolfgang Ketterle won the Nobel Prize in Physics for achieving BEC experimentally.

## 3. Thermal Changes & Latent Heat

* **Temperature Unit Conversion:**
  $$\text{K} = ^\circ\text{C} + 273.15 \quad (\text{Absolute Zero} = 0\text{ K} = -273.15^\circ\text{C})$$

* **Latent Heat (Hidden Heat):** Heat energy absorbed or released by a substance during a change of state **without any change in temperature**.
  1. **Latent Heat of Fusion ($L_f$):** Amount of heat required to convert $1\text{ kg}$ of solid into liquid at its melting point under atmospheric pressure.
     * Ice at $0^\circ\text{C}$ absorbs $334\text{ kJ/kg}$ (or $80\text{ cal/g}$) to become water at $0^\circ\text{C}$. Water at $0^\circ\text{C}$ has more energy than ice at $0^\circ\text{C}$.
  2. **Latent Heat of Vaporization ($L_v$):** Amount of heat required to convert $1\text{ kg}$ of liquid into gas at its boiling point under atmospheric pressure.
     * Water at $100^\circ\text{C}$ absorbs $2260\text{ kJ/kg}$ (or $540\text{ cal/g}$) to become steam at $100^\circ\text{C}$. **Steam causes more severe burns than boiling water** at $100^\circ\text{C}$ due to this extra latent heat!

* **Evaporation vs Boiling:**

| Feature | Evaporation | Boiling |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Type of Phenomenon** | Surface phenomenon | Bulk phenomenon |
| **Temperature** | Occurs at **any temperature below boiling point** | Occurs only at a specific **fixed Boiling Point** |
| **Effect** | **Causes cooling** (e.g., sweating, acetone on palm, water in earthen pot) | Does not cause cooling |
| **Factors Increasing Rate** | Surface area $\uparrow$, Temperature $\uparrow$, Wind speed $\uparrow$, Humidity $\downarrow$ | External Pressure $\uparrow \implies \text{BP}\uparrow$ |

## 4. Ideal Gas Behavior & Gas Laws

Ideal gas particles have negligible volume and zero intermolecular attractive forces.

$$\text{Ideal Gas Equation: } P V = n R T \quad (R = 8.314 \text{ J/K}\cdot\text{mol})$$

| Law | Mental Hook | Constant Variable | Mathematical Relation | Physical Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Boyle's Law** | **Boiling** | Temperature ($T$) | $P \propto \frac{1}{V} \implies P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2$ | Squeezing a sealed syringe reduces volume and increases pressure. |
| **Charles's Law** | **Hot Air Balloon** | Pressure ($P$) | $V \propto T \implies \frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$ | Heating air expands it, making hot air balloons rise. |
| **Gay-Lussac's Law** | **Pressure Cooker** | Volume ($V$) | $P \propto T \implies \frac{P_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{T_2}$ | Heating a rigid pressure cooker raises internal gas pressure. |
| **Avogadro's Law** | **Avogadro's Number** | Temp & Pressure ($P, T$) | $V \propto n$ | Equal volumes of all gases at same $T$ and $P$ contain equal number of molecules. |

* **Ideal Gas Kinetic Postulates:**
  1. Molecules move in continuous random straight-line motion.
  2. Speeds are not identical; they follow a **Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution**.
  3. Potential Energy of ideal gas molecules is **zero** ($PE = 0$).

## 5. Classification of Matter: Pure Substances vs Mixtures

```
                                  MATTER
                                     |
               +---------------------+---------------------+
               |                                           |
        PURE SUBSTANCE                                  MIXTURE
               |                                           |
       +-------+-------+                           +-------+-------+
       |               |                           |               |
   ELEMENTS        COMPOUNDS                  HOMOGENEOUS     HETEROGENEOUS
(Cu, Hg, Br2)   (H2O, NaCl, NaOH)              (Solutions)     (Colloids/Suspensions)
```

### A. Pure Substances
1. **Element:** Term coined by **Robert Boyle (1661)**; defined experimentally by **Antoine Lavoisier (1743)**. Basic form of matter that cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical reactions.
   * **Liquids at Room Temp ($25^\circ\text{C}$):** Mercury ($\text{Hg}$, metal) and Bromine ($\text{Br}_2$, non-metal).
   * **Liquids just above room temp ($>30^\circ\text{C}$):** Gallium ($\text{Ga}$) and Caesium ($\text{Cs}$).
   * **Gases:** 11 elements exist naturally as gases at room temp.
2. **Compound:** Formed by two or more elements **chemically combined in a fixed mass ratio** (e.g., $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ mass ratio $1:8$). Properties of a compound are entirely different from its constituent elements.

### B. True Solution, Colloidal Solution, and Suspension

| Property | True Solution | Colloidal Solution | Suspension |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Type** | Homogeneous mixture | Heterogeneous (looks homogeneous) | Heterogeneous mixture |
| **Particle Size** | $< 1\text{ nm}$ ($< 10^{-9}\text{ m}$) | $1\text{ nm} - 1000\text{ nm}$ | $> 1000\text{ nm}$ |
| **Visibility** | Invisible under microscope | Invisible to naked eye; visible under ultramicroscope | Visible to naked eye |
| **Tyndall Effect** | **Does not scatter light** (Path invisible) | **Scatters light beam** (Path visible) | Scatters light until particles settle |
| **Stability** | Very stable (Particles don't settle) | Stable (Particles don't settle) | Unstable (Particles settle at bottom) |
| **Separation** | Cannot filter | Separated by **Centrifugation** | Separated by ordinary Filtration / Decantation |
| **Examples** | Salt in water, Sugar in water, Brass, Iodine in alcohol (Tincture of Iodine) | Milk, Ink, Blood, Fog, Foam | Sand in water, Chalk powder in water, Muddy water |

### C. Types of Colloids (Dispersed Phase & Dispersion Medium)

| Dispersed Phase (Solute) | Dispersion Medium (Solvent) | Type of Colloid | Real-World Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Liquid** | **Gas** | Aerosol | Fog, Clouds, Mist |
| **Solid** | **Gas** | Aerosol | Smoke, Automobile exhaust |
| **Gas** | **Liquid** | Foam | Shaving cream, Soap suds |
| **Liquid** | **Liquid** | Emulsion | Milk, Face cream, Mayonnaise |
| **Solid** | **Liquid** | Sol | Milk of Magnesia ($Mg(OH)_2$), Mud |
| **Gas** | **Solid** | Foam | Sponge, Foam rubber, Pumice stone |
| **Liquid** | **Solid** | Gel | Jelly, Cheese, Butter |
| **Solid** | **Solid** | Solid Sol | Coloured gemstones, Milky glass |

## 6. Separation Methods for Mixtures

1. **Evaporation:** Separates a non-volatile solute from a volatile solvent (e.g., getting dye/pigment from ink).
2. **Centrifugation:** Rapid spinning forces denser particles to the bottom and lighter ones to the top (e.g., separating cream from milk, blood diagnostic tests, washing machine spin dryer).
3. **Separating Funnel:** Separates **immiscible liquids** based on density differences (e.g., kerosene oil and water, benzene and water).
4. **Sublimation:** Separates a sublimable solid from non-sublimable impurities (e.g., Ammonium chloride $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$, Camphor, Naphthalene, Anthracene, Iodine + Salt).
5. **Chromatography:** Separates solutes dissolved in the same solvent based on differing solubilities (e.g., separating dyes in black ink, natural pigments from flower petals, **detecting drugs in blood**).
6. **Distillation:** Separates miscible liquids with a large boiling point difference ($\ge 25\text{ K}$, e.g., Acetone and Water).
7. **Fractional Distillation:** Separates miscible liquids with boiling point difference $< 25\text{ K}$ using a fractionating column packed with glass beads (e.g., petroleum refining, separating gases from liquid air).
8. **Crystallization:** Purifies solids by cooling a saturated solution to form pure crystals (preferred over evaporation because it avoids charring/decomposition of solids).

## 7. Water Purification & Chemical Changes

### Municipal Water Treatment Sequence
$$\text{Reservoir} \rightarrow \text{Sedimentation Tank (solids settle)} \rightarrow \text{Loading Tank (Alum added)} \rightarrow \text{Filtration Tank (sand/gravel)} \rightarrow \text{Chlorination (kills germs)} \rightarrow \text{Homes}$$

* **Ion-Exchange Method:** Uses Cation ($H^+$) and Anion ($OH^-$) resins to yield **Pure Demineralized Water** free from all soluble salts.

### Physical vs Chemical Change Matrix

| Physical Change | Chemical Change |
| :--- | :--- |
| No new substance is formed | One or more new substances are formed |
| Reversible change | Irreversible change |
| Examples: Melting butter, cutting trees, boiling water, dissolving salt | Examples: Burning wood/paper, rusting iron, milk turning to curd, apple browning |

* **Special Case - Burning Candle:** Demonstrates **BOTH** physical change (melting of candle wax) and chemical change (burning of wax vapor and wick to form $\text{CO}_2$ and water vapor).

## 8. Solved PYQs (NDA / CDS / CAPF)

### Q1 (CDS II 2019)
**Question:** Which one of the following can extinguish fire more quickly?
*(a) Cold water | (b) Boiling water | (c) Hot water | (d) Ice*
* **Answer:** **(b) Boiling water**. Boiling water at $100^\circ\text{C}$ absorbs massive latent heat of vaporization ($2260\text{ kJ/kg}$) instantly and expands 1,700x into steam, displace oxygen and smothering flames.

### Q2 (NDA II 2024)
**Question:** People prefer to wear cotton clothes in summer because cotton clothes are:
*(a) good absorbers of water | (b) good conveyors of heat | (c) good radiators | (d) good absorbers of heat*
* **Answer:** **(a) good absorbers of water**. Cotton absorbs sweat, exposing it to air for rapid evaporation which causes cooling.

### Q3 (CAPF 2021)
**Question:** According to early Indian philosophers, Pancha Tattva includes:
*(a) Air, Earth, Fire, Sky, Water | (b) Air, Gold, Water, Fire, Earth | (c) Wood, Earth, Fire, Air, Water | (d) Air, Metal, Fire, Earth, Water*
* **Answer:** **(a) Air, Earth, Fire, Sky, Water**.

### Q4 (NDA II 2019)
**Question:** Tincture of iodine is an antiseptic solution. It contains:
*(a) Iodine in alcohol | (b) Iodine in water | (c) Potassium iodide in water | (d) Iodine in sodium hydroxide*
* **Answer:** **(a) 2-3% Iodine (solute) dissolved in alcohol and water (solvent)**.

### Q5 (CAPF 2020)
**Question:** Pure demineralized water free from all soluble mineral salts is obtained by:
*(a) Boiling | (b) Distillation | (c) Calgon process | (d) Ion-exchange resin method*
* **Answer:** **(d) Ion-exchange resin method**.
