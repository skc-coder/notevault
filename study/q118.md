# Question 118: Harmonic Progression Interchanged Terms Invariant

If the 7th term of a Harmonic Progression (HP) is 1/7 and the 10th term is 1/10, then what is its 17th term?

(a) $\frac{1}{17}$  
(b) $\frac{17}{70}$  
(c) $\frac{1}{70}$  
(d) $\frac{1}{27}$  

> [!faq]- View Solution
> **Step-by-Step Derivation (Method of $a$ and $d$):**  
> 1. By definition of HP, the reciprocals of HP terms form an Arithmetic Progression (AP).
>    - 7th term of AP ($A_7$) $= 7$
>    - 10th term of AP ($A_{10}$) $= 10$
> 
> 2. Set up AP general term equations ($A_k = a + (k-1)d$):
>    $$a + 6d = 7 \quad \text{--- (Eq 1)}$$
>    $$a + 9d = 10 \quad \text{--- (Eq 2)}$$
> 
> 3. Subtract Eq 1 from Eq 2:
>    $$3d = 3 \implies \mathbf{d = 1}$$
> 
> 4. Substitute $d = 1$ into Eq 1:
>    $$a + 6(1) = 7 \implies \mathbf{a = 1}$$
> 
> 5. Find 17th term of AP ($A_{17}$):
>    $$A_{17} = a + 16d = 1 + 16(1) = 17$$
> 
> 6. Reciprocal back to HP:
>    $$T_{17} = \frac{1}{A_{17}} = \frac{1}{17}$$
> 
> **General Invariant Shortcut:**  
> If $m$-th term of HP is $\frac{1}{m}$ and $n$-th term of HP is $\frac{1}{n}$, then the $(m+n)$-th term of HP is always $\frac{1}{m+n}$.
> 
> **Correct Answer:** **(a) $\frac{1}{17}$**
