# Atmosphere, Ozone Layer and Greenhouse Effect

## Theory, Intuition & Key Concepts

### 1. Atmospheric Composition & Layering

#### A. Chemical Composition of Atmosphere
- **Gas Percentage Breakdown (By Volume)**:
  $$\text{Nitrogen } (N_2 \approx 78.08\%) > \text{Oxygen } (O_2 \approx 20.95\%) > \text{Argon } (Ar \approx 0.93\%) > \text{Carbon Dioxide } (CO_2 \approx 0.04\%) > \text{Neon } (Ne \approx 0.0018\%)$$

> [!IMPORTANT] **Exam Distinction**:
> - Nitrogen ($78\%$) and Oxygen ($21\%$) make up **$99\%$** of atmospheric volume.
> - **Argon ($0.93\%$)** is the third most abundant gas, and the **most abundant noble gas**.

#### B. Vertical Thermal Layering

1. **Troposphere**:
   - Lowest layer extending from surface to **$8 \text{ km}$ at Poles and $18 \text{ km}$ at Equator** (thicker at equator due to strong convective currents).
   - Contains **$90\%$ of total air mass and all water vapor/clouds**.
   - All weather phenomena (rain, clouds, storms) occur HERE!
   - **Normal Lapse Rate**: Temperature decreases with height at a rate of **$6.5^\circ\text{C}$ per $1,000 \text{ m}$ ($1 \text{ km}$)** or **$1^\circ\text{C}$ per $165 \text{ meters}$**.
   - Upper boundary = **Tropopause** (temperature reaches $-80^\circ\text{C}$ over equator).

2. **Stratosphere**:
   - Extends from Tropopause up to **$50 \text{ km}$**.
   - **Ozone Layer ($O_3$)**: Concentrated between **$15-35 \text{ km}$** (Peak concentration at $\approx 22 \text{ km}$). Absorbs harmful UV radiation.
   - Temperature **increases with height** due to UV absorption by ozone (temperature inversion).
   - Zero weather turbulence $\to$ Ideal zone for flying jet aircraft!

3. **Mesosphere**:
   - Extends from $50 \text{ km}$ up to **$80 \text{ km}$**.
   - Temperature decreases with altitude, reaching **$-100^\circ\text{C}$ at Mesopause** (Coldest place in Earth's atmosphere!).
   - Meteors burn up in this layer due to atmospheric friction.

4. **Thermosphere / Ionosphere**:
   - Extends from $80 \text{ km}$ up to **$400 \text{ km}$**.
   - Contains electrically charged ions $\to$ Reflects radio waves back to Earth (enables radio communication).
   - Home to **Auroras** (Aurora Borealis in North Pole, Aurora Australis in South Pole).

5. **Exosphere**:
   - Uppermost layer merging into interplanetary space ($>400 \text{ km}$). Extremely rarefied gases (mostly Hydrogen & Helium).

### 2. Ozone Layer Depletion & International Conventions

#### A. Ozone Layer ($O_3$) Depletion Mechanics
- Ozone layer thickness is measured in **Dobson Units (DU)** ($1 \text{ DU} = 0.01 \text{ mm}$ thickness at STP).
- **Ozone Hole**: Defined as ozone thickness dropping below **$220 \text{ DU}$** over Antarctica during Austral spring (Sep-Oct).
- **Primary Ozone Depleting Substances (ODS)**: **Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)**, Halons, Carbon Tetrachloride, Methyl Chloroform.
- **Role of Polar Stratospheric Clouds (PSCs)**: In extreme Antarctic winter cold ($-80^\circ\text{C}$), PSCs provide ice crystal surfaces that convert inert chlorine reservoir species into reactive Chlorine free radicals ($Cl^\bullet$), which destroy $O_3$ molecules in spring sunlight ($1 \text{ } Cl^\bullet$ atom destroys $>100,000 \text{ } O_3$ molecules!).

#### B. International Protocols
1. **Vienna Convention (1985)**: Framework agreement for the protection of the ozone layer.
2. **Montreal Protocol (1987)**: Legally binding treaty to phase out production of Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) and ODS. Most successful global environmental treaty!
3. **Kigali Amendment (2016)**: Amendment to Montreal Protocol to phase down Hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs), which do not deplete ozone but are potent greenhouse gases.
