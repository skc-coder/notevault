# Heights and Distances

## Theory, Intuition & Formulas

Heights and Distances is a key practical application of trigonometry used to calculate physical heights, depths, and horizontal separations without direct physical measurement.

### Core Terminology & Definitions

- **Line of Sight**: The straight line segment drawn from the eye of an observer to the target object being observed.
- **Horizontal Line**: The line of sight parallel to the flat ground level passing through the observer's eye.
- **Angle of Elevation**: The acute angle formed between the horizontal line and the line of sight when the observer looks **upward** at an object located above eye level.
- **Angle of Depression**: The acute angle formed between the horizontal line and the line of sight when the observer looks **downward** at an object located below eye level.
  - **Fundamental Equality**: The angle of depression of object $B$ seen from point $A$ is numerically equal to the angle of elevation of point $A$ seen from object $B$ (due to alternate interior angles formed by parallel horizontal lines).

### Key Geometric Rules & Invariants

1. **Observer Distance vs. Angle of Elevation**:
   - As an observer moves closer to the base of a vertical structure (tower/building), the angle of elevation **increases**.
   - As an observer moves further away from the base, the angle of elevation **decreases**.
2. **Shadow Length vs. Sun Altitude**:
   - As the Sun's altitude (angle of elevation) decreases, the length of the shadow cast by a vertical post **increases**.
3. **Scale Invariance**:
   - If both the height of a tower and the horizontal distance to the observer are multiplied by the same scalar factor $k$, the angle of elevation remains **unchanged**.
