# Work, Energy and Power

Quantitative definition of scalar mechanical concepts, conservation principles, and Work-Energy Theorem.

---

## 1. Work ($W$)

Work is done by a force when the point of application undergoes displacement:
$$W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{s} = F s \cos\theta$$

- **Positive Work ($\theta < 90^\circ$)**: Force component acts in displacement direction (e.g., gravity on falling body).
- **Zero Work ($\theta = 90^\circ$ or $s = 0$)**: Force perpendicular to motion (e.g., centripetal force in circular orbit, carrying load horizontally).
- **Negative Work ($\theta > 90^\circ$)**: Force opposes displacement (e.g., friction, braking force).

---

## 2. Work-Energy Theorem

The net work done by all forces acting on a particle equals the change in its kinetic energy:
$$W_{\text{net}} = \Delta K = K_f - K_i = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 - \frac{1}{2} m u^2$$

---

## 3. Kinetic and Potential Energy

### Kinetic Energy ($K$)

Energy possessed by a body by virtue of its motion:
$$K = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 = \frac{p^2}{2m}$$

### Gravitational Potential Energy ($U$)

Energy possessed by virtue of position at height $h$:
$$U = m g h$$

---

## 4. Power ($P$)

Time rate of doing work:
$$P = \frac{dW}{dt} = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{v}$$

- **Units**:
  - SI Unit: Watt ($\text{W} = \text{J/s}$)
  - Horsepower: $1\text{ HP} = 746\text{ W}$
  - Commercial Energy: $1\text{ kWh} = 3.6 \times 10^6\text{ J}$
