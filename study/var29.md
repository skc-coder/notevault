# Variation 29: Price Increase with Expenditure-Consumption Compensation

## Problem Statement
When commodity price rises by $P\%$, prove that a consumer must reduce consumption by $\frac{P}{100+P} \times 100\%$ to keep budget outlay strictly constant.

---

## Proof & Theoretical Intuition

### 1. Multiplicative Product Balance
Let initial Price $= 100$, initial Consumption $= 100 \implies$ initial Expenditure $= 10000$.
- New Price $= 100 + P$.
- Let new Consumption be $C'$.
- Setting new Expenditure equal to initial Expenditure:
  $$(100 + P) \times C' = 10000 \implies C' = \frac{10000}{100 + P}$$
- Reduction in consumption:
  $$\Delta C = 100 - \frac{10000}{100 + P} = \frac{10000 + 100P - 10000}{100 + P} = \frac{100P}{100 + P}$$
- Expressing $\Delta C$ as a percentage of initial consumption $100$:
  $$\text{Percentage Reduction} = \left(\frac{P}{100 + P} \times 100\right)\%$$
