# Periodic Classification of Elements

Exhaustive chapter notes covering Early Triads & Octaves, Mendeleev's Periodic Table, Modern Periodic Law (Moseley), Block Classification ($s, p, d, f$), and Periodic Trends (Atomic Radius, Valency, Ionization Energy, Electronegativity, Metallic Character).

## 1. Early Attempts of Classification

* **Dobereiner's Triads (1817):** Arranged elements with similar properties into groups of three (**Triads**). The atomic mass of the middle element was roughly the arithmetic mean of the other two.
  * Triad Example 1: Lithium ($\text{Li} = 6.9$), Sodium ($\text{Na} = 23.0$), Potassium ($\text{K} = 39.1$). Mean $= (6.9 + 39.1)/2 = 23.0$.
  * Triad Example 2: Calcium ($\text{Ca} = 40.1$), Strontium ($\text{Sr} = 87.6$), Barium ($\text{Ba} = 137.3$).
  * Triad Example 3: Chlorine ($\text{Cl} = 35.5$), Bromine ($\text{Br} = 79.9$), Iodine ($\text{I} = 126.9$).
* **Newlands' Law of Octaves (1866):** Arranged elements in increasing order of atomic masses. Every $8\text{th}$ element had properties similar to the $1\text{st}$ element (like musical notes *Sa Re Ga Ma Pa Da Ni Sa*).
  * **Limitation:** Applicable only up to **Calcium ($\text{Ca}$)**. Assumed only 56 elements existed in nature.

## 2. Mendeleev's Periodic Table (1869)

* **Mendeleev's Periodic Law:** *"The physical and chemical properties of elements are a periodic function of their **Atomic Masses**."*
* Formulated based on hydrides and oxides of elements. Contained **6 Periods** and **8 Groups**.

### Successes of Mendeleev's Table
1. Left deliberate gaps for undiscovered elements and predicted their properties accurately:
   * **Eka-Boron** $\rightarrow$ Discovered later as **Scandium ($\text{Sc}$)**
   * **Eka-Aluminium** $\rightarrow$ Discovered later as **Gallium ($\text{Ga}$)**
   * **Eka-Silicon** $\rightarrow$ Discovered later as **Germanium ($\text{Ge}$)**
2. Accommodated Noble Gases when discovered without disturbing the table.

### Defects / Limitations
1. **Position of Hydrogen:** Hydrogen resembles both alkali metals (Group 1) and halogens (Group 17); no fixed position.
2. **Anomalous Pairs:** Placed Cobalt ($\text{Co} = 58.9$) before Nickel ($\text{Ni} = 58.7$); Tellurium ($\text{Te} = 127.6$) before Iodine ($\text{I} = 126.9$).
3. **Isotopes:** Could not explain positions of isotopes (which have different atomic masses but same atomic number).

## 3. Modern Periodic Table (Henry Moseley, 1913)

* **Modern Periodic Law:** *"The physical and chemical properties of elements are a periodic function of their **Atomic Numbers ($Z$)**."*
* **Structure:** Long form table designed by Bohr. Contains **18 Vertical Columns (Groups)** and **7 Horizontal Rows (Periods)**. Total 118 known elements.

```
       GROUPS 1-2                 GROUPS 13-18
        s-Block                      p-Block
     [Alkali & Earth]            [Non-Metals/Gases]
            |                            |
            +------- GROUPS 3-12 --------+
                      d-Block
                 [Transition Metals]
                         |
                 LANTHANIDES & ACTINIDES
                      f-Block
               [Inner Transition Metals]
```

### Block Classification ($s, p, d, f$)

1. **$s$-Block (Groups 1 & 2):** Valence electron enters $s$-subshell. Includes Alkali Metals ($\text{Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs}$) and Alkaline Earth Metals ($\text{Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba}$). General configuration: $ns^{1-2}$.
2. **$p$-Block (Groups 13 to 18):** Valence electron enters $p$-subshell. Contains Metals, Metalloids, Non-metals, Halogens (Group 17), and Noble Gases (Group 18). General configuration: $ns^2 np^{1-6}$.
   * **Representative Elements:** $s$-block and $p$-block elements together (excluding noble gases).
3. **$d$-Block (Groups 3 to 12):** Transition Metals. Incomplete inner $d$-subshell. Form colored ions, show variable oxidation states, and act as catalysts. General configuration: $(n-1)d^{1-10} ns^{1-2}$.
4. **$f$-Block (Bottom 2 Rows):** Inner Transition Metals.
   * **Lanthanides (4f-series):** 14 elements from Cerium ($Z=58$) to Lutetium ($Z=71$).
   * **Actinides (5f-series):** 14 elements from Thorium ($Z=90$) to Lawrencium ($Z=103$). All actinides are **radioactive**.

## 4. Periodic Trends Summary Table

How properties change across a Period (Left $\rightarrow$ Right) and down a Group (Top $\rightarrow$ Bottom).

| Property                              | Across a Period (Left to Right $\rightarrow$)          | Down a Group (Top to Bottom $\downarrow$)     | Reason / Cause                                                                                         |
| :------------------------------------ | :----------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Nuclear Charge ($Z_{\text{eff}}$)** | **Increases**                                          | **Increases**                                 | Protons added to nucleus                                                                               |
| **Atomic Radius / Size**              | **Decreases**                                          | **Increases**                                 | Across period: $Z_{\text{eff}}\uparrow$ pulls shell closer.<br>Down group: New shells added.           |
| **Valence Electrons**                 | **Increases** ($1 \rightarrow 8$)                      | **Remains Same**                              | Same group elements have identical outer valence electrons.                                            |
| **Valency**                           | **Increases $1 \rightarrow 4$, then decreases to $0$** | **Remains Same**                              | Depends on valence electrons ($8-e^-$).                                                                |
| **Ionization Energy / Enthalpy**      | **Increases**                                          | **Decreases**                                 | Energy to remove outer electron. Smaller size $\implies$ tighter hold.                                 |
| **Electron Affinity**                 | **Increases**                                          | **Decreases**                                 | Group 17 (Halogens) has highest EA. **Chlorine ($\text{Cl}$)** has highest EA!                         |
| **Electronegativity**                 | **Increases**                                          | **Decreases**                                 | Tendency to attract shared electron pair. **Fluorine ($\text{F}$)** is most electronegative ($4.0$)!   |
| **Metallic Character**                | **Decreases** (Metals $\rightarrow$ Non-metals)        | **Increases** (Elements become more metallic) | Ability to lose electrons ($e^-$).                                                                     |
| **Non-Metallic Character**            | **Increases**                                          | **Decreases**                                 | Ability to gain electrons ($e^-$).                                                                     |
| **Oxide Nature**                      | Basic $\rightarrow$ Amphoteric $\rightarrow$ Acidic    | Acidic character decreases / Basic increases  | Left oxides basic ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}$); Right oxides acidic ($\text{SO}_3, \text{Cl}_2\text{O}_7$). |

## 5. Solved PYQs (NDA / CDS / CAPF)

### Q1 (CDS 1 2024)
**Question:** Which one of the following properties decreases across the periodic table from left to right, and increases down a group?
*(a) Atomic radius | (b) Ionization energy | (c) Electronegativity | (d) Non-metallic character*
* **Answer:** **(a) Atomic radius**.

### Q2 (NDA II 2021)
**Question:** Which element has the highest electron affinity?
*(a) Fluorine | (b) Chlorine | (c) Bromine | (d) Iodine*
* **Answer:** **(b) Chlorine**. (Fluorine is most electronegative, but Chlorine has highest electron affinity due to small inter-electronic repulsion).

### Q3 (CAPF 2024)
**Question:** Eka-Aluminium predicted by Mendeleev was discovered later as:
*(a) Scandium | (b) Gallium | (c) Germanium | (d) Titanium*
* **Answer:** **(b) Gallium**.
