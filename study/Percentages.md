Let rate = p, quantity = q, and expenditure = pq (constant).
rate × quantity = price (or expenditure)
Assume:
- p increases by fraction x/y
- then new price = p(1 + x/y)

price (12 rs / 1 dz) * consumption (12 dz) = expenditure.

Since pq is constant:
p q = p(1 + x/y) · q'

⇒ q' = q / (1 + x/y)

⇒ q' = q · ( y / (x + y) )

So decrease in q:
q - q' = q [1 - y/(x+y)]  
= q [ x/(x+y) ]

⇒ fractional decrease = x/(x+y)

---

And multiplying the increase and decrease fraction (percentage) with the original values we get the actual increase or decrease, by definition.

# Population Growth and Decay Formulas

## Growth Scenario
- Original population: $P$
- Annual growth rate: $r\%$
- Time period: $n$ years

### Population after $n$ years
$$
\text{Population after } n \text{ years} = P \left(1 + \frac{r}{100}\right)^n
$$

### Increase in population
$$
\text{Increase} = P \left[\left(1 + \frac{r}{100}\right)^n - 1\right]
$$

---

## Decay Scenario
- Original population: $P$
- Annual decrease rate: $r\%$
- Time period: $n$ years

### Population after $n$ years
$$
\text{Population after } n \text{ years} = P \left(1 - \frac{r}{100}\right)^n
$$

### Decrease in population
$$
\text{Decrease} = P \left[1 - \left(1 - \frac{r}{100}\right)^n\right]
$$
