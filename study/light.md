### Definition

The **Absolute Refractive Index ($n$)** of a medium is the ratio of the speed of light in a vacuum ($c$) to its speed in that medium ($v$):

  

$$n = \frac{c}{v}$$

- **Unitless & Dimensionless** quantity.
    
      
    
- **Higher $n$** = Optically denser medium = Light travels **slower** and bends **more** towards the normal.

### 3 Formulas Frequently Asked in CDS Numericals

    $${^1n_2} = \frac{n_2}{n_1} = \frac{v_1}{v_2}$$

Here are the complete image formation tables for both **Convex (Converging)** and **Concave (Diverging)** lenses.

  

## 1. Convex Lens (Converging Lens)

A convex lens forms **real and inverted** images for most positions, but forms a **virtual and erect** image when the object is closer than the focal point ($F_1$).

  

|**Position of Object**|**Position of Image**|**Size of Image**|**Nature of Image**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**At infinity**|At focus $F_2$|Highly diminished, point-sized|Real and inverted|
|**Beyond $2F_1$**|Between $F_2$ and $2F_2$|Diminished|Real and inverted|
|**At $2F_1$**|At $2F_2$|Same size as object|Real and inverted|
|**Between $F_1$ and $2F_1$**|Beyond $2F_2$|Enlarged (Magnified)|Real and inverted|
|**At focus $F_1$**|At infinity|Infinitely large / Highly enlarged|Real and inverted|
|**Between $F_1$ and Optical Center ($O$)**|On the same side of the lens as object|Enlarged (Magnified)|**Virtual and erect**|

## 2. Concave Lens (Diverging Lens)

A concave lens **always** forms a **virtual, erect, and diminished** image, regardless of where the object is placed.

  

|**Position of Object**|**Position of Image**|**Size of Image**|**Nature of Image**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**At infinity**|At focus $F_1$|Highly diminished, point-sized|Virtual and erect|
|**Between infinity and Optical Center ($O$)**|Between focus $F_1$ and Optical Center ($O$)|Diminished|Virtual and erect|

### Quick Memory Tricks for CDS/Competitive Exams

- **Convex Lens $\approx$ Concave Mirror:** Their image formation patterns are identical in behavior (5 real cases, 1 virtual case).
    
      
    
- **Concave Lens $\approx$ Convex Mirror:** Always forms a **virtual, erect, and smaller (diminished)** image.
    
      
    
- **$2F$ is the symmetry point:** At $2F_1$, the image forms at $2F_2$ with the **same size** as the object.

Here is the breakdown of the **Lens Formula**, how it works, and key differences from the Mirror Formula based on your notes.

  

## 1. The Lens Formula

The Lens Formula relates object distance ($u$), image distance ($v$), and focal length ($f$) for thin spherical lenses:

  

$$\frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$$

- **$u$** = Distance of the object from the optical center ($O$). Always **negative ($-$)** for real objects.
    
      
    
- **$v$** = Distance of the image from the optical center ($O$).
    
      
    - **Positive ($+$)** if formed on the other side (Real Image).
        
          
        
    - **Negative ($-$)** if formed on the same side as object (Virtual Image).
        
          
        
- **$f$** = Focal length of the lens.
    
      
    - **Convex Lens:** Always **Positive ($+$)**
        
          
        
    - **Concave Lens:** Always **Negative ($-$)**
        
          
        

## 2. Lens Formula vs. Mirror Formula

The crucial difference stems from how light behaves: **Mirrors reflect** light back to the same side, while **Lenses refract** light through to the other side.

  

|**Feature**|**Mirror Formula**|**Lens Formula**|
|---|---|---|
|**Main Formula**|$\frac{1}{v} \mathbf{+} \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$|$\frac{1}{v} \mathbf{-} \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$|
|**Linear Magnification ($m$)**|$m = -\frac{v}{u}$|$m = +\frac{v}{u}$|
|**Real Image Location**|In front of mirror (same side as object) $\to v$ is **Negative**|Behind lens (opposite side of object) $\to v$ is **Positive**|
|**Convex Focal Length ($f$)**|**Positive ($+$)**|**Positive ($+$)**|
|**Concave Focal Length ($f$)**|**Negative ($-$)**|**Negative ($-$)**|

## 3. Power of Lens ($P$) & Combination Formulas

As highlighted in your notes:

  

1. **Power Formula:** Power is the reciprocal of focal length:
    
      
    
    $$P = \frac{1}{f \text{ (in meters)}} \quad \text{or} \quad P = \frac{100}{f \text{ (in cm)}}$$
    
    - **Unit:** Dioptre ($\text{D}$) or $\text{m}^{-1}$.
        
          
        
    - **Convex Lens:** $f > 0 \implies P > 0$ (**Positive Power**).
        
          
        
    - **Concave Lens:** $f < 0 \implies P < 0$ (**Negative Power**).
        
          
        
2. **Combination of Lenses in Contact:**
    
      
    - **Total Power ($P$):** Powers add up algebraically:
        
          
        
        $$P = P_1 + P_2 + P_3 + \dots$$
        
    - **Total Magnification ($m$):** Magnifications multiply together:
        
          
        
        $$m = m_1 \times m_2 \times m_3 \times \dots$$
        

## 4. Easy Trick to Remember the Sign Difference

- **Lens Formula Has a Minus Sign ($\mathbf{-}$):** Think of **L**ens = **L**ess (Subtraction $\to \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$).
    
      
    
- **Mirror Formula Has a Plus Sign ($\mathbf{+}$):** Think of **M**irror = **M**ore (Addition $\to \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$).
    
      
    
- **Magnification flips the sign:**
    
      
    - Lens formula has **$-$**, so Lens $m$ gets **$+$** ($m = \frac{v}{u}$).
        
          
        
    - Mirror formula has **$+$**, so Mirror $m$ gets **$-$** ($m = -\frac{v}{u}$).
- 
The sign of $m$ MUST match the orientation of the image:

- **Negative $m$** = Inverted (Real)
    
- **Positive $m$** = Upright (Virtual)
    

Because mirror real images form on the **same side** ($v$ and $u$ share the same sign), we must add a **$-$** to force $m$ to be negative. Because lens real images form on **opposite sides** ($v$ and $u$ have opposite signs), the ratio $\frac{v}{u}$ handles it automatically

**TL;DR — Optical Instruments Summary**

  

- **Periscope:**
    
      
    - **Setup:** 2 plane mirrors parallel to each other at **$45^\circ$**.
        
          
        
    - **Principle:** Uses the **Law of Reflection** to turn light rays twice ($90^\circ$ each) to see over obstacles.
        
          
        
- **Compound Microscope:**
    
      
    - **Setup:** 2 Convex lenses (**Objective + Eyepiece**).
        
          
        
    - **Principle:** Objective creates a magnified intermediate real image; eyepiece magnifies it further for viewing tiny, close objects.
        
          
        
- **Astronomical Telescope:**
    
      
    - **Setup:** 2 Convex lenses (**Objective with large focal length $f \uparrow$ + Eyepiece**).
        
          
        
    - **Principle:** Large objective gathers maximum light from far-away objects (stars/planets) to create a focal image, which the eyepiece magnifies (image is inverted).
        
          
        
- **Terrestrial Telescope:**
    
      
    - **Setup:** 3 Convex lenses (**Objective + Erecting Lens + Eyepiece**).
        
          
        
    - **Principle:** The middle **erecting lens** flips the upside-down image right-side up without changing its size so land objects can be viewed normally.

### **Explanation:**

The defect described in the question—where a person can see nearby objects clearly but cannot see distant objects clearly—is **Myopia** (nearsightedness).

  

In a myopic eye, rays of light coming from distant objects focus **in front of the retina** instead of directly on it.

  

#### **Evaluating the Statements:**

1. **Excessive curvature of the eye lens (Correct):**
    
      
    - Increased curvature makes the lens thicker and increases its converging power, causing distant light rays to bend too sharply and focus _in front_ of the retina.
        
          
        
2. **The eyeball has become too small (Incorrect):**
    
      
    - If the eyeball is too small (short), the image forms _behind_ the retina. This causes **Hypermetropia** (farsightedness), not myopia.
        
          
        
3. **The focal length of the eye lens is too large (Incorrect):**
    
      
    - A larger focal length means less converging power, which also causes hypermetropia. In myopia, the focal length of the eye lens is actually **too short**.
        
          
        
4. **Elongation of the eyeball (Correct):**
    
      
    - If the eyeball is longer than normal along its optical axis, the distance between the eye lens and the retina increases, so the image forms _in front_ of the retina.
        
          
        

### **Summary of Causes for Myopia:**

- **Excessive curvature of the eye lens** (Statement 1)
    
      
    
- **Elongation of the eyeball** (Statement 4)

In **farsightedness (Hypermetropia)**, the situation is the exact opposite of myopia. A person can see distant objects clearly, but nearby objects appear blurry.

  

Here is how the anatomy works in hypermetropia:

  

### **Causes of Farsightedness**

1. **Shortening of the Eyeball:**
    
      
    - The front-to-back distance of the eyeball is **too short** (decreased internal distance between the lens and retina).
        
          
        
    - Because the retina sits closer to the lens than normal, light rays from nearby objects don't have enough room to converge in time—they attempt to form a focus **behind the retina**.
        
          
        
2. **Insufficient Curvature / Too Large Focal Length:**
    
      
    - The eye lens is too flat (not curved enough) or lacks sufficient bending power.
        
          
        
    - A flatter lens has a **longer focal length**, meaning it cannot bend light sharply enough to focus it on the screen (retina) in front of it.
        

### **Comparing the Two**

| **Feature**           | **Nearsightedness (Myopia)**           | **Farsightedness (Hypermetropia)**      |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Clear Vision**      | Near objects                           | Distant objects                         |
| **Eyeball Shape**     | **Elongated** (too long front-to-back) | **Shortened** (too short front-to-back) |
| **Lens Focal Length** | Too short (over-converging)            | **Too long** (under-converging)         |
| **Focus Point**       | In **front** of the retina             | **Behind** the retina                   |
| **Correction Lens**   | **Concave** (diverging) lens           | **Convex** (converging) lens            |

A **curved (thick) lens** has more focusing power.
