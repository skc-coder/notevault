# CDS Chapter 14 Q49

## Problem Statement
For what values of $k$ will $4x^5 + 9x^4 - 7x^3 - 5x^2 - 4kx + 3k^2$ contain $(x - 1)$ as a factor?
- (a) $\frac{3}{2}, -1$
- (b) $3, -1$
- (c) $0, \frac{1}{3}$
- (d) $\frac{1}{3}, 1$

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Apply Factor Theorem
Let $P(x) = 4x^5 + 9x^4 - 7x^3 - 5x^2 - 4kx + 3k^2$.
Since $(x - 1)$ is a factor of $P(x)$, by the Factor Theorem:
$$P(1) = 0$$

### Step 2: Evaluate $P(1)$
Substitute $x = 1$ into $P(x)$:
$$P(1) = 4(1)^5 + 9(1)^4 - 7(1)^3 - 5(1)^2 - 4k(1) + 3k^2 = 0$$
$$4 + 9 - 7 - 5 - 4k + 3k^2 = 0$$

Combine numerical constants:
$$(4 + 9 - 7 - 5) - 4k + 3k^2 = 0$$
$$1 - 4k + 3k^2 = 0$$

Rearranging into standard quadratic form in $k$:
$$3k^2 - 4k + 1 = 0$$

### Step 3: Factorize Quadratic Equation in $k$
Split middle term $-4k = -3k - k$:
$$3k^2 - 3k - k + 1 = 0$$
$$3k(k - 1) - 1(k - 1) = 0$$
$$(3k - 1)(k - 1) = 0$$

Therefore:
$$3k - 1 = 0 \implies k = \frac{1}{3}$$
$$k - 1 = 0 \implies k = 1$$

---

## Final Answer
- **Correct Option**: **(d) $\frac{1}{3}, 1$**
