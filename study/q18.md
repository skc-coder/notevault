# Question 18: HCF of (a + b) and (a - b) for Co-prime (a, b) (CDS 2014 I)

For any integers $a$ and $b$ with $\operatorname{HCF}(a, b) = 1$, what is $\operatorname{HCF}(a+b, a-b)$ equal to?

(a) It is always 1  
(b) It is always 2  
(c) Either 1 or 2  
(d) None of these  

> [!faq]- View Solution
> ### Rigorous Mathematical Proof:
> Let $g = \operatorname{HCF}(a+b, a-b)$.
> By definition of HCF, $g$ divides both $(a+b)$ and $(a-b)$:
> 1. $g \mid \Big((a+b) + (a-b)\Big) \implies g \mid 2a$.
> 2. $g \mid \Big((a+b) - (a-b)\Big) \implies g \mid 2b$.
> 
> So $g$ is a common divisor of $2a$ and $2b$.  
> Therefore, $g \mid \operatorname{HCF}(2a, 2b) = 2 \cdot \operatorname{HCF}(a, b)$.
> 
> Since $\operatorname{HCF}(a, b) = 1$:
> $$g \mid (2 \times 1) \implies \mathbf{g \mid 2}$$
> 
> > ⚠️ **CRITICAL NOTATION NOTICE**:  
> > The symbol $\mathbf{g \mid 2}$ is read as **"$g$ divides $2$"** (or **"$g$ is a divisor of $2$"**).  
> > It does **NOT** mean "$2$ divides $g$"!  
> > 
> > What numbers divide $2$? The positive factors/divisors of $2$ are **$1$ and $2$**.  
> > Therefore, $g$ can be **either $1$ or $2$**!
> 
> ---
> 
> ### Intuitive Parity Breakdown (Why HCF can be 1 or 2):
> 
> The value of $\operatorname{HCF}(a+b, a-b)$ depends on the **parity (Odd or Even)** of $a$ and $b$:
> 
> 1. **Case 1: One number is ODD, one number is EVEN** (e.g. $a = 9, b = 8$)
>    - $a + b = \text{Odd} + \text{Even} = \mathbf{\text{Odd}}$
>    - $a - b = \text{Odd} - \text{Even} = \mathbf{\text{Odd}}$
>    - The numbers $(a+b)$ and $(a-b)$ are **both ODD**. 
>    - Since both are odd, **2 CANNOT divide them!**
>    - Therefore, the HCF **cannot be 2**, so it **MUST be 1**!
> 
> 2. **Case 2: Both numbers are ODD** (e.g. $a = 23, b = 17$)
>    - $a + b = \text{Odd} + \text{Odd} = \mathbf{\text{Even}}$
>    - $a - b = \text{Odd} - \text{Odd} = \mathbf{\text{Even}}$
>    - The numbers $(a+b)$ and $(a-b)$ are **both EVEN**.
>    - Since both are even, **2 definitely divides both of them!**
>    - Therefore, the HCF is **2**.
> 
> *(Note: Both numbers CANNOT be even because $\operatorname{HCF}(a,b) = 1$, so 2 cannot divide both $a$ and $b$.)*
> 
> **Conclusion:** Depending on whether one is even or both are odd, the HCF is **either 1 or 2**.
> 
> **Correct Answer:** **(c) Either 1 or 2**
