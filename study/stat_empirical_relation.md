# Empirical Relation & Skewness

## Theory & Mathematical Properties

### 1. Karl Pearson's Empirical Relationship
For moderately skewed (asymmetric) unimodal frequency distributions, the relationship between Mean ($\bar{x}$), Median ($M_e$), and Mode ($M_o$) is given by:

$$\text{Mode} = 3(\text{Median}) - 2(\text{Mean})$$

Alternatively expressed as:
$$\text{Mean} - \text{Mode} = 3(\text{Mean} - \text{Median})$$

---

### 2. Distribution Types based on Symmetry

#### A. Symmetric Distribution
- When data is perfectly symmetrical about the center:
  $$\text{Mean} = \text{Median} = \text{Mode}$$
- Skewness is zero ($S_k = 0$).

#### B. Positively Skewed Distribution (Right-Skewed)
- Tail extends to the right (higher values).
- Relationship:
  $$\text{Mean} > \text{Median} > \text{Mode}$$

#### C. Negatively Skewed Distribution (Left-Skewed)
- Tail extends to the left (lower values).
- Relationship:
  $$\text{Mode} > \text{Median} > \text{Mean}$$
