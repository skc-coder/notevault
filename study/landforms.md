# Geomorphic Processes and Landforms

Comprehensive, exhaustive master concept notes on Fluvial, Aeolian, Glacial, Karst, and Coastal Erosional and Depositional Landforms synthesized from Pages 104-146 of the Physical Geography PDF.

---

## Theory, Intuition & Key Concepts

### 1. Fluvial Landforms (Running Water)

#### A. Fluvial Erosional Landforms
1. **V-Shaped Valleys & Gorges / Canyons**: Carved by vertical river incision in youth stage (e.g., Grand Canyon - Colorado river).
2. **Waterfalls & Rapids**: Sudden vertical drop in river bed over resistant hard rock.
3. **Plunge Pools & Potholes**: Circular depressions carved in river bed by eddy currents and pebbles.
4. **River Terraces**: Stepped terraces marking former floodplain levels.
5. **Meanders & Oxbow Lakes**: S-shaped river loops in mature/old stage $\to$ cut off forms an **Oxbow Lake**.

#### B. Fluvial Depositional Landforms
1. **Alluvial Fans & Cones**: Fan-shaped deposits at the foothills (bhabar region) where river exits mountains.
2. **Floodplains & Natural Levees**: Raised embankments along river banks formed by repeated flood deposition.
3. **Deltas**: Triangular deposits at river mouth (e.g., Sundarbans Delta - largest in world).

---

### 2. Aeolian Landforms (Wind & Arid Deserts)

#### A. Aeolian Erosional Landforms
1. **Mushroom Rocks (Pedestal Rocks)**: Lower portion undercut faster by wind-blown sand.
2. **Yardangs**: Parallel steep-sided ridges of hard rock aligned with prevailing wind direction.
3. **Zeugen**: Table-shaped landforms formed in horizontal alternating hard/soft rock layers.
4. **Deflation Hollows / Blowouts**: Depressions created by wind blowing away loose sand.
5. **Inselbergs**: Isolated residual rock hills standing abruptly out of desert plains.

#### B. Aeolian Depositional Landforms
1. **Sand Dunes**:
   - **Barchans**: Crescent-shaped dunes with horns pointing **downwind / leeward**.
   - **Seif Dunes**: Long longitudinal dune ridges parallel to wind direction.
   - **Transverse Dunes**: Dunes perpendicular to wind direction.
2. **Loess**: Fine unstratified yellow dust/silt blown over vast distances (e.g., Loess Plateau, China).

---

### 3. Glacial Landforms (Moving Ice)

#### A. Glacial Erosional Landforms
1. **Cirque / Corrie / Cwm**: Arm-chair shaped steep-backed hollow carved by mountain glaciers.
2. **Arete**: Sharp knife-edge ridge separating two adjacent cirques.
3. **Horn / Glacial Peak**: Pyramidal peak formed when 3 or more cirques erode backwards (e.g., Matterhorn in Alps).
4. **Glacial Trough / U-Shaped Valley**: Flat-bottomed steep-sided valley formed by valley glaciers.
5. **Fjords**: Steep-sided submerged glacial troughs along coasts (e.g., Norway coast).
6. **Roche Moutonnée**: Asymmetrical rock hill with smooth stoss (up-glacier) side and steep plucked leeward side.

#### B. Glacial Depositional Landforms
1. **Moraines**: Unsorted glacial debris deposits (Lateral, Medial, Terminal moraines).
2. **Eskers**: Long sinuous ridges of sand/gravel deposited by subglacial meltwater streams.
3. **Drumlins**: Smooth oval egg-shaped hills of till ("Basket of Eggs Topography").

---

### 4. Karst Topography (Groundwater Erosion in Limestone)

#### A. Karst Erosional Landforms
1. **Sinkholes & Doline**: Circular funnel-shaped depressions on limestone surfaces.
2. **Lapis**: Irregular grooves and ridges formed by solution.
3. **Caves & Caverns**: Underground hollows formed by carbonic acid dissolving calcium carbonate ($CaCO_3$).

#### B. Karst Depositional Landforms
1. **Stalactites**: Icicle-like calcium carbonate deposits hanging down from cave roofs (*c-tites* = ceiling).
2. **Stalagmites**: Calcium carbonate pillars growing up from cave floors (*g-mites* = ground).
3. **Pillars / Columns**: Joined stalactite and stalagmite.
