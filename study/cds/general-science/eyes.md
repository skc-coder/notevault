## 1. Outer & Protective Layers

- **Cornea:** A thin, bulging membrane made of proteins and cells that covers the front of the eye. It performs **most of the light refraction** before light reaches the lens.
    
      
    
- **Sclera:** The tough, white outer protective wall of the eyeball.
    
      
    
- **Choroid:** The middle vascular layer between the sclera and retina that supplies nutrients and prevents light reflection inside the eye.
    
      
    

## 2. Light Regulation & Focusing System

- **Iris:** A dark, muscular diaphragm behind the cornea that **controls the size of the pupil**.
    
      
    
- **Pupil:** The central opening created by the iris that regulates the **amount of light entering the eye**.
    
      
    
- **Crystalline Lens:** A transparent, **convex** lens made of a fibrous, jelly-like material. It forms a **real and inverted** image on the retina and merely fine-tunes/adjusts focal length.
    
      
    
- **Ciliary Muscles:** Muscles connected to the lens that **control its curvature and focal length** (allowing accommodation; human eye focal length is roughly $2.5\text{ cm}$).
    
      
    
- **Aqueous Humour:** A watery fluid filling the gap between the cornea and the lens.
    
      
    
- **Vitreous Humour:** A clear gel filling the large space between the lens and the retina, keeping the eyeball spherical.
    
      
    

## 3. Sensory Layer (Retina) & Processing

- **Retina:** The light-sensitive inner screen where the image is formed. It contains specialized light-sensitive photoreceptor cells:
    
      
    - **Rods:** Responsible for **evening/dim-light vision**.
        
          
        
    - **Cones:** Responsible for **daylight vision and color perception**.
        
          
        
- **Fovea:** A central region on the retina densely packed with **cones**, providing maximum visual acuity and resolution.
    
      
    
- **Blind Spot:** The area where the optic nerve exits the eye; contains **no photoreceptors**, so no image is perceived here.
    
      
    
- **Optic Nerve:** Transmits electrical signals generated by photoreceptors directly to the **brain**.
    
      
    

## 4. Key Distance Limits

- **Near Point (Least Distance of Distinct Vision):** $25\text{ cm}$ (for a normal young adult eye).
    
      
    
- **Far Point:** $\infty$ (Infinity).

## 1. Light Adjustment (Iris & Pupil Control)

The **Iris** controls the size of the **Pupil** to adjust the amount of light entering the eye depending on environmental brightness:

  

- **In a Bright Room:**
    
      
    
    $$\text{Iris contracts pupil} \longrightarrow \text{Pupil opens less} \longrightarrow \text{Less light enters}$$
    
    - **Intuition:** Protects the retina from harsh light overload.
        
          
        
- **In Dim Light / Darkness:**
    
      
    
    $$\text{Iris expands pupil} \longrightarrow \text{Pupil opens more} \longrightarrow \text{More light enters}$$
    
    - **Intuition:** Gathers as much light as possible so you can see in low-light conditions.
        
          
        

## 2. Power of Accommodation (Ciliary Muscles)

**Power of Accommodation** is the ability of the eye lens to adjust its focal length ($f$) so that images of objects at varying distances always focus sharply onto the retina.

  

This process is controlled entirely by the **Ciliary Muscles**:

  

### Case A: Viewing Distant Objects (Far Vision)

$$\text{Ciliary Muscles RELAX} \longrightarrow \text{Lens becomes THIN} \longrightarrow \text{Curvature } \downarrow \text{ and Focal Length } \uparrow \longrightarrow \text{Focuses Distant Objects}$$

- **Intuition:** Light rays from far away are nearly parallel and don't need sharp bending. A thin, relaxed lens has **low power** and a **longer focal length**, allowing distant images to form on the retina effortlessly.
    
      
    

### Case B: Viewing Nearby Objects (Near Vision)

$$\text{Ciliary Muscles CONTRACT} \longrightarrow \text{Lens becomes THICKER} \longrightarrow \text{Curvature } \uparrow \text{ and Focal Length } \downarrow \longrightarrow \text{Focuses Nearby Objects}$$

- **Intuition:** Light rays coming from close objects spread out sharply (divergent) and require high bending power to focus. By contracting, the ciliary muscles make the lens bulge and get thicker, increasing its **bending power** and shortening its **focal length** ($f \downarrow$).



**TL;DR — Defects of Vision**

  

- **Cataract:** Lens turns **milky/cloudy** in old age $\to$ Fixed via **Surgery**.
    
      
    
- **Myopia (Nearsightedness):** Can't see **far** (image forms **in front** of retina due to long eyeball/short focal length) $\to$ Fixed via **Concave Lens**.
    
      
    
- **Hypermetropia (Farsightedness):** Can't see **near** (image forms **behind** retina due to small eyeball/long focal length) $\to$ Fixed via **Convex Lens**.
    
      
    
- **Presbyopia:** Aging weakens ciliary muscles $\to$ Fixed via **Bifocal Lenses** (Upper: Concave for far | Lower: Convex for near).
