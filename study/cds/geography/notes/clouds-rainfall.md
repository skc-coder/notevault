# Condensation, Clouds and World Rainfall

Comprehensive, exhaustive master concept notes on Atmospheric Humidity, Dew Point, Condensation Forms (Fog, Mist, Dew, Frost), Cloud Classification, Rainfall Types, and Precipitation Dynamics synthesized from Pages 185-204 of the Physical Geography PDF.

## Theory, Intuition & Key Concepts

### 1. Humidity & Condensation Forms

#### A. Humidity Definitions
- **Absolute Humidity**: Actual weight of water vapor per unit volume of air ($\text{g/m}^3$).
- **Specific Humidity**: Weight of water vapor per unit weight of moist air ($\text{g/kg}$).
- **Relative Humidity (RH)**: Ratio of actual water vapor present in air to the maximum water vapor capacity of air at that temperature, expressed as a percentage:
  $$\text{Relative Humidity (RH)} = \frac{\text{Actual Water Vapor Capacity}}{\text{Maximum Water Vapor Capacity at } T} \times 100\%$$
- **Saturated Air**: Air holding $100\%$ Relative Humidity.
- **Dew Point**: Temperature at which a given parcel of air becomes completely saturated ($RH = 100\%$).

#### B. Forms of Condensation
Condensation occurs when air temperature drops below Dew Point:
1. **Dew**: Water droplets deposited on cold surfaces (grass, leaves) when Dew Point is **above freezing point ($>0^\circ\text{C}$)** under clear, calm night skies.
2. **Frost**: Ice crystals deposited on cold surfaces when Dew Point is **at or below freezing point ($\le 0^\circ\text{C}$)**.
3. **Fog**: Condensation in air near Earth's surface creating suspended water droplets that reduce visibility to **$<1 \text{ km}$**.
4. **Mist**: Similar to fog, but contains higher moisture and visibility is between **$1 \text{ km} - 2 \text{ km}$**.
5. **Smog**: Mixture of **Smoke + Fog**.
   - *Classical Smog*: Photochemical/reducing smog in cold humid climates ($SO_2$).
   - *Photochemical Smog (Los Angeles Smog)*: Formed by action of sunlight on nitrogen oxides ($NO_x$) and hydrocarbons $\to$ yields Ozone ($O_3$), PAN (Peroxyacetyl nitrate), and formaldehyde.

### 2. Cloud Classification & High/Low Clouds

Clouds are classified according to altitude into **4 Major Families**:

```text
High Clouds   (8,000 m - 12,000 m)  ---> Cirrus, Cirrocumulus, Cirrostratus
Middle Clouds (2,000 m - 7,000 m)   ---> Altocumulus, Altostratus
Low Clouds    (0 m - 2,000 m)       ---> Stratocumulus, Stratus, Nimbostratus
Vertical Growth Clouds              ---> Cumulus, Cumulonimbus
```

#### A. Detailed Cloud Types & Key Identifiers
1. **Cirrus (High)**: Feathery, wispy, mare's tail appearance composed entirely of **ice crystals**. Zero rain!
2. **Cirrostratus (High)**: Thin, whitish sheet covering sky. Creates a **Halo effect** around the Sun and Moon due to light refraction in ice crystals!
3. **Cirrocumulus (High)**: Small white patches or ripples in sky ("Mackerel Sky").
4. **Altocumulus (Middle)**: Fluffy grey/white woolly rolls or waves ("Sheep Back Clouds").
5. **Altostratus (Middle)**: Continuous grey/blue sheet producing watery sun appearance and light rain/drizzle.
6. **Nimbostratus (Low)**: Dark, thick, amorphous rain clouds producing continuous steady rain/snow.
7. **Cumulus (Vertical)**: Dense, cotton-wool appearance with flat base and dome-shaped top. Fair weather cloud.
8. **Cumulonimbus (Vertical - Thunderstorm Cloud)**:
   - Massive, towering mountain-like cloud with characteristic **Anvil-shaped top** extending up to Tropopause.
   - Causes violent **thunderstorms, lightning, torrential downpours, squalls, and Hailstones**!

### 3. Types of Rainfall & Distribution

1. **Convectional Rainfall**:
   - Thermal origin. Intense solar heating of land $\to$ air expands, rises, cools adiabatically $\to$ heavy 4 o'clock showers in Equatorial regions.
2. **Orographic (Relief) Rainfall**:
   - Occurs when moist air is forced to ascend over a mountain barrier.
   - **Windward Side**: Heavy rainfall as air ascends and cools.
   - **Leeward Side**: Descending air warms and expands $\to$ dry zone called **Rain Shadow Region** (e.g., Deccan Plateau behind Western Ghats).
3. **Cyclonic / Frontal Rainfall**:
   - Occurs along fronts when warm air ascends over cold air mass.
