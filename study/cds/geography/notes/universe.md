## 1. Theories on the Origin of the Universe & Solar System

### A. Origin of the Universe
1. **Big Bang Theory (Expanding Universe Hypothesis)**:
   - **Formulated by**: Georges Lemaître (1927).
   - **Supported/Expanded by**: Edwin Hubble (1929 - proved redshift and expansion of galaxies) & George Gamow (1948 - Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation prediction).
   - **Core Concept**:
     - **Singularity (Tiny Ball)**: ~13.7 to 13.8 billion years ago, all matter in the universe existed in one tiny point (singular atom) with infinitesimally small volume, infinite temperature, and infinite density.
     - **Violent Explosion**: The tiny ball exploded violently, initiating a period of extremely rapid inflation within fractions of a second, followed by steady expansion that continues today.
     - **Cooling & Atom Formation**: Within 3 minutes of the Big Bang, the first atoms (Hydrogen and Helium) began to form. Within 300,000 years, temperature dropped to ~4,500 K (or ~3,000 K), rendering the universe transparent and releasing **CMBR (Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation)**.
   - **Experimental Verification**:
     - **CERN (European Organization for Nuclear Research)**: Large Hadron Collider (LHC) in Geneva recreated early-universe conditions to detect the Higgs Boson ("God Particle").
     - **WMAP / COBE (NASA)** & **Planck Satellite (ESA)**: Mapped Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation anisotropy across the sky.

2. **Alternative / Historical Theories**:
   - **Steady State Theory**: Proposed by Fred Hoyle. Asserted that the universe has no beginning or end and remains constant in density due to continuous creation of matter. (Largely disproved by CMBR detection).

---

### B. Origin of Earth & Solar System (Early vs Modern Hypotheses)

1. **Early Hypotheses (Monistic / Evolutionary)**:
   - **Nebular Hypothesis**:
     - **Immanuel Kant (1755)** & revised by **Pierre-Simon Laplace (1796)**.
     - **Mechanism**: Planets formed out of a cloud of gas and dust (Nebula) associated with a youthful, slowly rotating Sun. Rotational speed increased, centrifugal force overcame gravity, and rings of matter detached to form planets.

2. **Catastrophic / Dualistic (Biparental) Hypotheses**:
   - **Planetesimal Hypothesis**: Chamberlain and Moulton (1900). A wandering star approached the Sun, pulling out a cigar-shaped extension of solar material via tidal force.
   - **Tidal Hypothesis**: Sir James Jeans (1919) and Harold Jeffreys (1929). Further refined the cigar-shaped filament (filamentous ejection) contracting into planetesimals.
   - **Binary Theories**: Russell, Lyttleton, and Hoyle. Posited a companion star to the Sun to account for angular momentum distribution in the Solar System.

3. **Modern Revisionist Hypotheses**:
   - **Interstellar Dust Hypothesis**: Otto Schmidt (1943 - Russia) & Carl Weizsäcker (Germany). Solar nebula was enriched with dust and gas. Gravitational accretion and collisions of particles formed **planetesimals**, which coalesced into proto-planets.

## 2. Stellar Evolution, Life Cycle of Stars & Dark Matter

### A. Formation of Stars & Galaxies
1. **Galaxy**: A vast system containing billions of stars, dust clouds, and interstellar gas held together by gravity.
   - **Milky Way Galaxy (Akash Ganga)**: Spiral galaxy, diameter ~80,000 to 100,000 light-years. The Solar System is situated ~27,000 light-years from the Galactic Centre.
   - **Light Year ($ly$)**: Distance light travels in 1 Julian year. $1\text{ ly} = 9.46 \times 10^{12}\text{ km} = 9.46 \times 10^{15}\text{ m}$.
   - **Astronomical Unit ($AU$)**: Average distance from Earth to Sun. $1\text{ AU} \approx 149.6 \text{ million km} \approx 1.496 \times 10^{8}\text{ km}$.
   - **Parsec ($pc$)**: Parallactic second. $1\text{ pc} \approx 3.26\text{ ly} \approx 3.08 \times 10^{13}\text{ km}$.

2. **Stellar Life Cycle**:
   - **Protostar / Nebula**: Accumulation of Hydrogen gas cloud gravitational collapse $\rightarrow$ Core heating initiates Nuclear Fusion ($4\text{H} \rightarrow \text{He} + \text{Energy}$).
   - **Main Sequence Star**: Fusion pressure balances gravitational collapse (Sun is currently in Main Sequence, age ~4.6 billion years, remaining ~5 billion years).
   - **Red Giant Phase**: Hydrogen in core exhausts $\rightarrow$ core contracts, outer envelope expands and cools.

### B. End States & The Chandrasekhar Limit
- **Chandrasekhar Limit ($1.44 M_{\odot}$)**: Formulated by Nobel Laureate Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar. Maximum mass of a stable white dwarf star.
  1. **If Initial Core Mass $\le 1.44 M_{\odot}$**:
     - Star sheds outer layers into a planetary nebula.
     - Core collapses into a **White Dwarf** (Degenerate Carbon-Oxygen core supported by electron degeneracy pressure).
     - Eventually cools into a **Black Dwarf**.
  2. **If Mass $> 1.44 M_{\odot}$ up to $\sim 3 M_{\odot}$**:
     - Undergoes violent **Supernova Explosion**.
     - Core contracts into a **Neutron Star** (rapidly rotating pulsar emitting regular radio pulses).
  3. **If Mass $> 3 M_{\odot}$**:
     - Gravitational collapse overcomes neutron degeneracy pressure.
     - Collapses into a **Black Hole** (singularity of infinite density and gravity; light cannot escape event horizon).

### C. Cosmic Energy Balance
- **Visible Matter**: ~4.9% of total universe mass-energy.
- **Dark Matter**: ~26.8% (non-baryonic, non-luminous matter exerting gravitational attraction).
- **Dark Energy**: ~68.3% (repulsive force driving accelerated expansion of the universe).

## 3. Structure & Atmospheric Zones of the Sun

### A. Internal Structure
1. **Core**: Inner region (0 to $0.25 R_{\odot}$). Temperature ~15,000,000 °C ($1.5 \times 10^7\text{ K}$). Extreme density ($150\text{ g/cm}^3$). High-pressure nuclear fusion of H into He.
2. **Radiative Zone**: Energy transfers outwards via photon radiation/scattering ($0.25$ to $0.7 R_{\odot}$).
3. **Convective Zone**: Hot plasma rises and cool plasma sinks in convection cells ($0.7$ to $1.0 R_{\odot}$).

### B. Solar Atmosphere & Solar Wind Phenomena
1. **Photosphere**: Visible surface of the Sun. Temperature ~5,500 °C to 6,000 K. Features **Sunspots** (cooler magnetic regions ~3,800 K with an 11-year solar cycle).
2. **Chromosphere**: Middle atmospheric layer (~2,000 km thick), visible as a pinkish ring during total solar eclipses.
3. **Corona**: Outermost aura of plasma extending millions of kilometers. Temperature reaches 1,000,000 °C to 3,000,000 °C. Visible during total solar eclipse or using a coronagraph.
4. **Solar Wind & Auroras**:
   - **Solar Wind**: Stream of charged particles (protons & electrons) escaping corona into heliosphere.
   - **Interaction with Earth Atmosphere**: Geomagnetic field deflects solar wind toward poles. Charged particles excite oxygen and nitrogen gas in the Ionosphere:
     - **Aurora Borealis**: Northern Lights (North Pole / Arctic).
     - **Aurora Australis**: Southern Lights (South Pole / Antarctic).

## 4. Planetary Classification & Physical Parameters

### A. Terrestrial vs Jovian Planets

| Parameter | Terrestrial Planets (Inner) | Jovian Planets (Outer) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Members** | Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars | Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune |
| **Location** | Orbit inside Asteroid Belt | Orbit outside Asteroid Belt |
| **Composition** | Metallic core, silicate rock shell | Hydrogen, Helium, Ammonia, Methane ices |
| **Density** | High density ($\ge 3.9 \text{ g/cm}^3$, Earth = $5.51 \text{ g/cm}^3$) | Low density ($\le 1.63 \text{ g/cm}^3$, Saturn = $0.69 \text{ g/cm}^3$) |
| **Atmosphere** | Thin, secondary atmosphere (lost primordial H/He) | Thick primordial atmosphere retained due to high gravity & cold temp |
| **Satellites** | Few to none (Mercury/Venus=0, Earth=1, Mars=2) | Extensive ring systems & dozens of moons (Saturn=146+, Jupiter=95+) |

---

### B. Individual Planetary Profiles & Unique Anomalies

1. **Mercury**:
   - Smallest planet, closest to Sun. No atmosphere (extreme temperature diurnal range: +430°C day, -180°C night).
   - Zero natural satellites. Fastest orbital period (~88 days).

2. **Venus ("Earth's Twin")**:
   - **Retrograde Rotation**: Rotates Clockwise (East to West) on its axis, unlike most planets.
   - **Runaway Greenhouse Effect**: 96.5% $\text{CO}_2$ atmosphere with dense clouds of sulfuric acid. Hottest planet (~465°C continuous).
   - **Rotation vs Revolution**: Rotation period (243 Earth days) is longer than its orbital period (225 Earth days).
   - Brightest natural object in night sky after Moon ("Morning Star" / "Evening Star").

3. **Earth**:
   - Density: $5.517 \text{ g/cm}^3$ (highest in Solar System).
   - Shape: **Oblate Spheroid (Geoid)** — Equatorial diameter ($12,756 \text{ km}$) > Polar diameter ($12,714 \text{ km}$) by ~42 km due to centrifugal force of **axial rotation**.

4. **Mars ("Red Planet")**:
   - Reddish color due to **Iron Oxide ($\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$)** on surface.
   - Satellites: **Phobos** and **Deimos**.
   - Landmarks: **Olympus Mons** (largest shield volcano in Solar System), **Valles Marineris** (giant canyon system).

5. **Jupiter**:
   - Largest planet by mass and volume.
   - Features: **Great Red Spot** (anticyclonic storm running for centuries).
   - Moons: **Ganymede** (largest natural satellite in Solar System, larger than Mercury), Callisto, Io (volcanically active), Europa (subsurface ocean).

6. **Saturn**:
   - **Lowest Density**: $0.687 \text{ g/cm}^3$ (less dense than water; would float in an ocean).
   - Spectacular planetary ring system made of ice and rock dust.
   - Moons: **Titan** (has dense nitrogen atmosphere and liquid methane/ethane lakes), Enceladus (ice geysers). Largest moon count in solar system (146+ confirmed).

7. **Uranus**:
   - **Extreme Axial Tilt ($97.8^{\circ}$)**: "Sideways planet" / "Rolling planet".
   - **Retrograde Rotation**: Rotates Clockwise (East to West), along with Venus.
   - Methane gas in atmosphere absorbs red light, giving it a cyan/blue-green appearance.

8. **Neptune**:
   - Furthest planet from Sun. Coldest planetary core temperatures.
   - Deep blue color due to atmospheric methane. Extremely high-speed planetary winds ($> 2,000 \text{ km/h}$).
   - Moon: **Triton** (retrograde orbit around Neptune).

## 5. Orbital Dynamics, Lagrange Points & Earth Mechanics

### A. Sun-Earth System & Lagrange Points ($L_1$ to $L_5$)
- **Lagrange Points**: Locations in space where the gravitational forces of two large bodies (e.g. Sun and Earth) exactly equal the centrifugal force required for a small object to move with them.
  - **$L_1$ (Lagrange 1)**: Located ~1.5 million km inside Earth's orbit toward Sun. Uninterrupted view of Sun. (Home of **Aditya-L1** solar observation satellite & SOHO).
  - **$L_2$**: Located ~1.5 million km directly behind Earth. Ideal for deep space telescopes (Home of **James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)**).
  - **$L_3$**: Located behind the Sun opposite Earth's orbit.
  - **$L_4$ & $L_5$**: Stable triangular points $60^{\circ}$ ahead and behind Earth in its orbital plane (Trojan point traps).

### B. Planetary Density & Satellite Rankings (PYQ Focus)
1. **Planetary Density Sequence (Descending Order)**:
   $$\text{Earth } (5.52 \text{ g/cm}^3) > \text{Mercury } (5.43) > \text{Venus } (5.24) > \text{Mars } (3.93) > \text{Neptune } (1.64) > \text{Jupiter } (1.33) > \text{Uranus } (1.27) > \text{Saturn } (0.69)$$
2. **Moons Count**: Saturn (146+) > Jupiter (95) > Uranus (28) > Neptune (16) > Mars (2) > Earth (1) > Mercury = Venus (0).

## 6. PYQ Analysis & Detailed Explanations

### Question 1 (NDA 2019)
**Ques**: Which one of the following hypothesis/theory explains the origin of the universe?
- (a) Nebular hypothesis
- (b) Binary theory
- (c) Big bang theory
- (d) Planetesimal hypothesis

**Correct Answer**: **(c) Big bang theory**
- **Explanation**: The Nebular hypothesis, Binary theory, and Planetesimal hypothesis explain the origin of the **Solar System / Planets**, whereas the **Big Bang Theory** (Lemaître, 1927) specifically explains the origin and expansion of the **Universe**.

### Question 2 (CDS 2019)
**Ques**: What is a constellation?
- (a) A particular pattern of equidistant stars from the earth in the sky
- (b) A particular pattern of stars that may not be equidistant from the earth and sky
- (c) A particular pattern of planets of our solar system in the sky
- (d) A particular pattern of stars, planets and satellites in the sky due to their position in space

**Correct Answer**: **(b) A particular pattern of stars that may not be equidistant from the earth and sky**
- **Explanation**: A constellation is a recognizable 2D pattern of stars projected on the celestial sphere as seen from Earth. The stars making up a constellation are at vastly different actual light-year distances from Earth; they only appear adjacent due to line-of-sight alignment.

### Question 3 (CDS 2020)
**Ques**: Which of the following groups of planets is termed as 'gas planets' (Jovian) as they are composed primarily of lighter ices, liquids and gases?
- (a) Mars, Jupiter, Neptune, Uranus
- (b) Jupiter, Uranus, Neptune, Saturn
- (c) Saturn, Mars, Jupiter, Neptune
- (d) Neptune, Saturn, Mars, Uranus

**Correct Answer**: **(b) Jupiter, Uranus, Neptune, Saturn**
- **Explanation**: Jovian planets (gas and ice giants) comprise Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. Mars is a rocky terrestrial planet, eliminating options (a), (c), and (d).

### Question 4 (CDS 2021)
**Ques**: Which one of the following is the correct sequence of arrangement of the given planets in descending order of their density (in $\text{gm/cm}^3$)?
- (a) Earth > Jupiter > Venus > Saturn
- (b) Jupiter > Earth > Saturn > Venus
- (c) Earth > Venus > Jupiter > Saturn
- (d) Earth > Venus > Saturn > Jupiter

**Correct Answer**: **(c) Earth > Venus > Jupiter > Saturn**
- **Explanation**: Earth ($5.52\text{ g/cm}^3$) > Venus ($5.24\text{ g/cm}^3$) > Jupiter ($1.33\text{ g/cm}^3$) > Saturn ($0.69\text{ g/cm}^3$).

### Question 5 (CDS 2022)
**Ques**: In a group discussion on shape and size of the Earth, three students stated the following points:
1. Student 1: The shape of the Earth is basically an oblate spheroid.
2. Student 2: The polar diameter of the Earth is more than the equatorial diameter.
3. Student 3: Bulge along the equatorial region is due to revolution of the Earth.

Who among the above students is/are correct?
- (a) Student 1 only
- (b) Student 1 and Student 2 only
- (c) Student 2 and Student 3 only
- (d) Student 1, Student 2 and Student 3

**Correct Answer**: **(a) Student 1 only**
- **Explanation**:
  - Statement 1 is correct: Earth is an oblate spheroid (geoid).
  - Statement 2 is incorrect: Equatorial diameter ($12,756\text{ km}$) is GREATER than polar diameter ($12,714\text{ km}$).
  - Statement 3 is incorrect: Equatorial bulge is caused by the centrifugal force of Earth's **AXIAL ROTATION**, NOT its orbital revolution around the Sun.
