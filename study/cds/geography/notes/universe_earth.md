# Physical & World Geography: Universe, Solar System & Earth

## Theory, Intuition & Key Concepts

### Universe & Galaxies
- **Cosmology**: The study of the Universe.
- **Milky Way (Akash Ganga)**: Spiral galaxy containing about 300 billion stars, including our Sun.
- **Andromeda**: Spiral galaxy, closest major galaxy to the Milky Way.
- **Galaxy Classifications**:
  - **Spiral**: Central nucleus with sweeping arms (e.g., Milky Way, Andromeda).
  - **Elliptical**: Smooth, featureless, lacking spiral arms.
  - **Irregular**: Lack distinct symmetry or shape.

### Key Astronomical Theories & Discoveries
- **Geocentric Model**: Proposed by **Ptolemy** (AD 140) placing Earth at the center.
- **Heliocentric Model**: Proposed by **Copernicus** (1543) establishing the Sun at the center. Supported by **Kepler** (specified Sun at center of solar system).
- **Galaxies Beyond Milky Way**: Demonstrated by **Edwin Hubble** (1924).
- **Origin Theories**:
  - **Big Bang Theory**: Proposed by **Georges Lemaître** (explosion occurred ~13.8 billion years ago).
  - **Steady State Theory**: Proposed by **Bondi, Gold, and Fred Hoyle**.
  - **Pulsating Theory**: Proposes alternate expansion and contraction phases.

## Complete Cheatsheet: Names, Events, Terms & Plans

### 1. Key Historical Names & Astronomers
| Name | Key Contribution / Discovery |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ptolemy** (AD 140) | Proposed Geocentric Theory (Earth at center of Universe). |
| **Copernicus** (1543) | Proposed Heliocentric Theory (Sun at center of Universe). |
| **Kepler** | Formulated laws of planetary motion; placed Sun at center of Solar System. |
| **Edwin Hubble** (1924) | Proved existence of galaxies outside the Milky Way. |
| **Georges Lemaître** | Proposed the **Big Bang Theory** for origin of the Universe. |
| **Bondi, Gold & Fred Hoyle** | Proposed the **Steady State Theory**. |
| **William Herschel** (1781) | Discovered planet **Uranus**. |
| **J.G. Galle** (1846) | Discovered planet **Neptune**. |

### 2. Major Space Events, Missions & Phenomena
| Event / Phenomenon | Description / Key Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Big Bang** | ~13.8 billion years ago cosmic explosion initiating the Universe. |
| **Solar Flare** | Bouts of hot ionized gas emitted by the Sun; poses danger to satellite communication. |
| **Aurora** | Solar wind particles trapped by Earth's magnetic field; **Aurora Borealis** (North) and **Aurora Australis** (South). |
| **Meteor / Meteorite** | Meteoroid entering atmosphere creates fiery trail (**Meteor/Shooting Star**); impact rock on Earth is **Meteorite**. |
| **Equinoxes** | Equal day and night (Sun overhead at Equator): **Vernal Equinox** (21st March), **Autumnal Equinox** (23rd September). |
| **Solstices** | **Summer Solstice** (21st June, Sun overhead at Tropic of Cancer, longest day in N. Hemisphere); **Winter Solstice** (22nd December, Sun overhead at Tropic of Capricorn). |
| **Perigee & Apogee** | Moon closest to Earth (**Perigee**: 356,000 km); Moon farthest (**Apogee**: 407,000 km). |
| **Perihelion & Aphelion** | Earth closest to Sun (**Perihelion**: 3rd Jan, ~147 M km); Earth farthest (**Aphelion**: 4th July, ~152 M km). |
| **Mars Space Missions** | Vikings, Pathfinder, Mars Odyssey, Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM / Mangalyaan). |

### 3. Solar System Quick Reference & Planetary Superlatives
| Property / Term | Celestial Body / Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Largest Planet** | Jupiter (Lord of the Heavens, fastest rotation 9.8 hrs) |
| **Smallest Planet** | Mercury (fastest revolution 88 days, highest diurnal temp range) |
| **Hottest Planet & Earth's Twin** | Venus (97% $CO_2$, 70% albedo, clockwise rotation East to West) |
| **Red Planet** | Mars (Phobos & Deimos satellites) |
| **Least Dense Planet** | Saturn (30x less dense than Earth, Titan satellite with nitrogen atmosphere) |
| **Green Planet** | Uranus (inclined 98°, rotates North to South) & Neptune (Methane presence) |
| **Largest Satellite** | Ganymede (Jupiter) |
| **Smallest Satellite** | Deimos (Mars) |
| **Brightest Star** | Sirius (Dog Star) |
| **Closest Star to Solar System** | Proxima Centauri |
| **Blue Moon** | Second full Moon occurring in a single calendar month |
| **Sunspot Periodicity** | 11-year cycle of dark, cold regions on Sun's surface |
