# Geomorphology and Earth Interior

Comprehensive, exhaustive master concept notes on the interior structure of the Earth, crustal composition, seismic wave dynamics, discontinuities, and direct/indirect sources of geological data synthesized from Pages 20-35 of the Physical Geography PDF.

---

## Theory, Intuition & Key Concepts

### 1. Sources of Information about Earth's Interior

#### A. Direct Sources
1. **Surface Rock Samples**: Mining rocks and surface observations (e.g., deep gold mines in South Africa like Mponeng Gold Mine reaching $3-4 \text{ km}$ depth).
2. **Deep Earth Drilling Projects**:
   - **Deep Ocean Drilling Project**: Drilling oceanic crust.
   - **Kola Superdeep Borehole (Russia)**: Deepest artificial point on Earth, reaching $12,262 \text{ m}$ ($12.2 \text{ km}$). Temperatures reached $180^\circ\text{C}$, halting deeper drilling.
3. **Volcanic Eruptions**: Magma brought to the surface from depths of $\approx 100-200 \text{ km}$ (Asthenosphere) provides direct chemical samples of the upper mantle.

#### B. Indirect Sources
1. **Meteors / Meteorites**: Extra-terrestrial solid bodies formed from the same primordial nebular material as Earth. Core compositions of iron-nickel meteorites mirror Earth's core.
2. **Seismic Waves**: Analysis of earthquake body wave propagation ($P$-waves and $S$-waves) through different rock densities.
3. **Gravity Anomaly**: Variations in gravitational acceleration ($g$) across the globe due to uneven mass distribution ($g$ is higher at the poles than at the equator due to centrifugal force and Earth's flattened polar radius).
4. **Magnetic Field & Palaeomagnetism**: Earth's geomagnetic field generated by liquid outer core dynamo action.
5. **Temperature & Pressure Gradients**: Temperature and pressure increase systematically with depth ($\approx 1^\circ\text{C}$ per $32 \text{ meters}$ in the upper crust).

### 2. Earth's Interior Layering & Chemical Composition

#### A. Chemical Classification (Suess Model: SIAL, SIMA, NIFE)
- **SIAL (Crust)**: Rich in **Silica ($Si$) & Aluminium ($Al$)**. Light granitic rocks. Density $\approx 2.7 \text{ g/cm}^3$.
- **SIMA (Mantle)**: Rich in **Silica ($Si$) & Magnesium ($Mg$)**. Dense basaltic rocks. Density $\approx 3.5-5.5 \text{ g/cm}^3$.
- **NIFE (Core)**: Rich in **Nickel ($Ni$) & Iron ($Fe$)**. High density metallic core. Density $\approx 11-13 \text{ g/cm}^3$.

---

#### B. Mechanical Layering (Crust, Mantle, Core)

##### 1. The Crust
- **Outermost thin solid shell**.
- **Oceanic Crust**: Thin ($\approx 5-10 \text{ km}$ thick), dense ($\approx 3.0 \text{ g/cm}^3$), composed primarily of **Basalt** (rich in silica, iron, and magnesium).
- **Continental Crust**: Thick ($\approx 35-70 \text{ km}$ thick, up to $70-100 \text{ km}$ beneath major mountain ranges like the Himalayas), less dense ($\approx 2.7 \text{ g/cm}^3$), composed primarily of **Granite**.

##### 2. The Mantle
- Extends from the base of the crust down to a depth of $2,900 \text{ km}$. Makes up $\approx 84\%$ of Earth's volume and $67\%$ of Earth's mass.
- **Asthenosphere**:
  - Upper portion of the mantle extending from $\approx 80-200 \text{ km}$ depth.
  - Highly ductile, semi-fluid / plastic state ("weak zone" — *Astheno* means weak).
  - Main source of magma during volcanic eruptions.
  - Tectonic plates of the Lithosphere float atop the ductile Asthenosphere.
- **Lithosphere**: Rigid outer layer comprising the **Crust + Uppermost Solid Mantle** ($\approx 10-200 \text{ km}$ thick).

##### 3. The Core
- Extends from $2,900 \text{ km}$ down to the center of Earth ($6,371 \text{ km}$).
- **Outer Core**: Liquid state (prohibited from transmitting $S$-waves). Generates Earth's magnetic field via geodynamo action.
- **Inner Core**: Solid state due to immense overburden pressure, despite extreme temperature ($\approx 5500-6000^\circ\text{C}$).

### 3. Seismic Waves & Discontinuities

#### A. Seismic Waves Breakdown
1. **Body Waves**: Travel through Earth's interior.
   - **Primary ($P$) Waves**: Longitudinal / compressional waves (particles vibrate parallel to wave direction). Fastest waves ($v \approx 6-13 \text{ km/s}$). Travel through **solids, liquids, and gases**.
   - **Secondary ($S$) Waves**: Transverse / shear waves (particles vibrate perpendicular to wave direction). Slower ($v \approx 3.5-7 \text{ km/s}$). Travel **ONLY through solids** (blocked by liquid outer core).
2. **Surface Waves ($L$-waves & Rayleigh waves)**: Travel along Earth's surface. Slowest, highest amplitude, cause maximum destruction.

#### B. Internal Discontinuities
Boundary zones where seismic wave velocity changes abruptly due to density shifts:

```text
Upper Crust  --- Conrad Discontinuity --- Lower Crust
Crust        --- Mohorovičić (Moho) Discontinuity --- Upper Mantle
Upper Mantle --- Repetti Discontinuity --- Lower Mantle
Mantle       --- Gutenberg Discontinuity --- Outer Core
Outer Core   --- Lehmann Discontinuity --- Inner Core
```

1. **Conrad Discontinuity**: Between Upper Granitic Crust and Lower Basaltic Crust.
2. **Mohorovičić (Moho) Discontinuity**: Between Crust and Upper Mantle.
3. **Repetti Discontinuity**: Between Upper Mantle and Lower Mantle.
4. **Gutenberg Discontinuity**: Between Lower Mantle and Liquid Outer Core ($2,900 \text{ km}$).
5. **Lehmann Discontinuity**: Between Liquid Outer Core and Solid Inner Core ($5,150 \text{ km}$).

### 4. Elemental Abundance Breakdown

#### A. Abundance of Elements in Entire Earth (By Mass)
$$\text{Iron } (Fe \approx 32.1\%) > \text{Oxygen } (O \approx 30.1\%) > \text{Silicon } (Si \approx 15.1\%) > \text{Magnesium } (Mg \approx 13.9\%) > \text{Sulfur } (S \approx 2.9\%) > \text{Nickel } (Ni \approx 1.8\%)$$

#### B. Abundance of Elements in Earth's Crust (By Mass)
$$\text{Oxygen } (O \approx 46.6\%) > \text{Silicon } (Si \approx 27.7\%) > \text{Aluminium } (Al \approx 8.1\%) > \text{Iron } (Fe \approx 5.0\%) > \text{Calcium } (Ca \approx 3.6\%) > \text{Sodium } (Na \approx 2.8\%)$$

> [!IMPORTANT] **Exam Distinction / Trap**:
> - Most abundant element in **Entire Earth**: **Iron ($Fe$)**.
> - Most abundant element in **Earth's Crust**: **Oxygen ($O$)**.
> - Most abundant **Metal** in Earth's Crust: **Aluminium ($Al$)**.
