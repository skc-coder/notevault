# Problem: Sequential Ratio Changes

> [!question] Problem Statement
> Two numbers are initially in the ratio $P : Q$. 
> - When $1$ is added to both the numerator and the denominator, the ratio changes to $R/S$.
> - Again, when $1$ is added to both the numerator and the denominator, the ratio becomes $1/2$. 
> 
> **Find the sum of $P$ and $Q$.**

***

> [!example] Solution Approach
> Let's analyze the condition directly relating the initial state to the final state.
> 
> We added $1$ twice to both the numerator and the denominator, so we have effectively added $2$ to both initial values $P$ and $Q$.
> 
> This gives us the following equation:
> $$ \frac{P + 2}{Q + 2} = \frac{1}{2} $$
> 
> Cross-multiplying to solve:
> $$ 2(P + 2) = 1(Q + 2) $$
> $$ 2P + 4 = Q + 2 $$
> $$ 2P - Q = -2 $$

> [!warning] Key Insight
> Notice that we only have a **single linear equation** with two variables ($P$ and $Q$). 
> 
> Because of this, we cannot uniquely determine the exact values of $P$ and $Q$, nor can we directly calculate their sum ($P + Q$). 
> 
> **Conclusion:** To find the correct answer, we must evaluate the provided **multiple-choice options** to see which pair of $(P, Q)$ satisfies the equation $2P - Q = -2$.
