# Common Names, Chemical Formulas & Industrial Processes


These processes are frequently tested to check your knowledge of industrial chemistry, specific products, and their required catalysts.

  

| **Process Name**     | **Product Manufactured** | **Chemical Formula** | **Key Catalyst / Reagent**       |
| -------------------- | ------------------------ | -------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Haber Process        | Ammonia                  | $NH_3$               | Iron ($Fe$)                      |
| Contact Process      | Sulphuric Acid           | $H_2SO_4$            | Vanadium Pentoxide ($V_2O_5$)    |
| Ostwald Process      | Nitric Acid              | $HNO_3$              | Platinum ($Pt$)                  |
| Solvay Process       | Washing Soda             | $Na_2CO_3$           | Uses brine, $NH_3$, and $CaCO_3$ |
| Bayer Process        | Alumina (from Bauxite)   | $Al_2O_3$            | Sodium Hydroxide ($NaOH$)        |
| Hall-Héroult Process | Aluminium                | $Al$                 | Cryolite (lowers melting point)  |
| Chlor-Alkali Process | Sodium Hydroxide         | $NaOH$               | Electrolysis of aqueous $NaCl$   |

### Sublimable Solids

These are solids that transition directly into a gas when heated. Exam questions almost always ask how to separate these from non-sublimable substances (like salt or sand). The answer is always **sublimation**.

  

| **Substance**     | **Chemical Formula** | **Key Exam Context**                                                                               |
| ----------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ammonium Chloride | $NH_4Cl$             | Frequently paired with sodium chloride ($NaCl$) in questions. Heating leaves $NaCl$ behind.        |
| Iodine            | $I_2$                | Sublimates to form distinct violet vapors. Often paired with sand or salt in separation questions. |
| Naphthalene       | $C_{10}H_8$          | The material in mothballs; sublimates at room temperature over time.                               |
| Camphor           | $C_{10}H_{16}O$      | Used in similar contexts to naphthalene for separation questions.                                  |
| Anthracene        | $C_{14}H_{10}$       | Another classic sublimable organic solid.                                                          |

### Important Liquid Mixtures & Solutions

Questions here focus on whether liquids mix completely (miscible) or form layers (immiscible), which determines how you separate them.

  

| **Mixture / Solution**      | **Components**       | **Type**                 | **Separation Method / Key Concept**                                                      |
| --------------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Acetone + Water             | Acetone + $H_2O$     | Miscible Liquids         | **Simple Distillation.** Boiling point difference is significant ($> 25^\circ\text{C}$). |
| Alcohol + Water             | Ethanol + $H_2O$     | Miscible Liquids         | **Fractional Distillation.** Boiling point difference is small ($< 25^\circ\text{C}$).   |
| Tincture of Iodine          | Iodine + Alcohol     | Solid in Liquid Solution | Acts as a true solution. Used as an antiseptic.                                          |
| Carbon Disulphide + Sulphur | $CS_2$ + $S$         | True Solution            | Forms a homogeneous mixture because sulphur is completely soluble in $CS_2$.             |
| Kerosene/Oil + Water        | Hydrocarbon + $H_2O$ | Immiscible Liquids       | **Separating Funnel.** Oil floats on water.                                              |
| Chloroform + Water          | $CHCl_3$ + $H_2O$    | Immiscible Liquids       | **Separating Funnel.** Chloroform is denser and forms the bottom layer.                  |
