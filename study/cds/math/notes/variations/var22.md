# Variation 22: Moving Aircraft Angular Acceleration & Speed

## Concept & Kinematics Formula

An aircraft flying horizontally at a constant altitude $H$ with constant velocity $v$ passes directly over a tracking radar station. When the angle of elevation of the aircraft changes from $\alpha$ to $\beta$ in time interval $\Delta t$, the uniform ground speed $v$ is given by:

$$v = \frac{H(\cot \beta - \cot \alpha)}{\Delta t}$$

---

## Numerical Application

**Scenario**: An aircraft flying horizontally at an altitude of $1\text{ km} = 1000\text{ m}$ is observed at an elevation of $60^\circ$. After $10\text{ s}$, its elevation reduces to $30^\circ$. Calculate its uniform speed in $\text{km/h}$.

1. Ground distance covered in $10\text{ s}$:
   $$d = 1000(\cot 30^\circ - \cot 60^\circ) = 1000\left(\sqrt{3} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right) = \frac{2000}{\sqrt{3}}\text{ m}$$

2. Speed in $\text{m/s}$:
   $$v = \frac{2000/\sqrt{3}\text{ m}}{10\text{ s}} = \frac{200}{\sqrt{3}}\text{ m/s}$$

3. Convert to $\text{km/h}$:
   $$v_{\text{km/h}} = \frac{200}{\sqrt{3}} \times \frac{18}{5} = \frac{720}{\sqrt{3}} = 240\sqrt{3}\text{ km/h}$$
