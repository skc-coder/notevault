# Complementary Angles Theorem for Height

## Theory & Algebraic Proof

If the angles of elevation of the top of a tower of height $h$ from two points on the ground at distances $a$ and $b$ ($a > b$) from its base are complementary ($\theta$ and $90^\circ - \theta$):

### Analytical Proof:
1. In the outer right triangle of base $a$:
   $$\tan(90^\circ - \theta) = \frac{h}{a} \implies \cot \theta = \frac{h}{a} \implies \tan \theta = \frac{a}{h}$$
2. In the inner right triangle of base $b$:
   $$\tan \theta = \frac{h}{b}$$
3. Equating the two expressions for $\tan \theta$:
   $$\frac{a}{h} = \frac{h}{b} \implies h^2 = ab \implies h = \sqrt{ab}$$
