# Mixture Evaporation & Solution Adulteration

## Theory & Proofs

### 1. Invariant Solute Conservation Principle
In solution evaporation or water addition problems, the amount of pure solute remains constant before and after evaporation/addition:
$$\text{Volume}_1 \times \text{Concentration}_1\% = \text{Volume}_2 \times \text{Concentration}_2\%$$

### 2. Adulteration Dilution Equation
If an adulterated solution of volume $V$ contains $x\%$ impurity, total impurity amount is $V \cdot \frac{x}{100}$.
To reduce impurity concentration to $y\%$ by adding pure constituent $A$:
$$\frac{\text{Impurity Volume}}{\text{New Total Volume}} = \frac{y}{100}$$
$$\frac{V \cdot \frac{x}{100}}{V + A} = \frac{y}{100} \implies V \cdot x = y(V + A) \implies A = V \left(\frac{x - y}{y}\right)$$
