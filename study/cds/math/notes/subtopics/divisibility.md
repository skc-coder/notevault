# Divisibility Rules & Mathematical Proofs

## 1. Divisibility Rule of 7
Let $N = 10A + B$. $N \equiv 0 \pmod 7 \iff A - 2B \equiv 0 \pmod 7$.

---

## 2. Divisibility Rule of 11
Alternating sum of digits: $N \equiv a_0 - a_1 + a_2 - a_3 + \dots \pmod{11}$.

---

## 3. Divisibility Rule of 13
$N = 10A + B \equiv 0 \pmod{13} \iff A + 4B \equiv 0 \pmod{13}$.

---

## 4. Combined 7-11-13 Shortcut (3-Digit Grouping Rule)
Since $7 \times 11 \times 13 = 1001 \equiv -1 \pmod{1000}$, alternating sum of 3-digit groups $G_0 - G_1 + G_2 - \dots$ determines divisibility by 7, 11, and 13.
