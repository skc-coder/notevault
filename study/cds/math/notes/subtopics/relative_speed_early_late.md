# Relative Speed & Early/Late Arrival Theorems

## 1. Relative Speed Fundamentals

Relative speed is the speed of one body with respect to another moving body.

- **Opposite Direction Motion**:
  $$S_{\text{rel}} = x + y$$
- **Same Direction Motion**:
  $$S_{\text{rel}} = |x - y|$$
- **Time to Meet / Overtake**:
  $$T_{\text{meet}} = \frac{\text{Initial Separation Distance}}{S_{\text{rel}}}$$

## 2. Early and Late Arrival Distance Formulae

### Case 1: Reaches Late at Speed $x$, Early at Speed $y$
When traveling from $A$ to $B$ at speed $x\text{ km/h}$, a person is $t_1$ hours late. At speed $y\text{ km/h}$, the person is $t_2$ hours early.

$$\text{Distance } D = \frac{x \cdot y}{y - x} \times (t_1 + t_2)$$

### Case 2: Reaches Late in Both Journeys
When traveling at speed $x\text{ km/h}$, a person is $t_1$ hours late. Upon increasing speed by $y\text{ km/h}$ (new speed = $x + y$), the person is still $t_2$ hours late.

$$\text{Distance } D = \frac{x(x + y)}{y} \times (t_1 - t_2)$$

### Case 3: Fractional Speed Change & Original Usual Time
If a person travels at $\frac{x}{y}$ of their usual original speed and reaches $t$ minutes late (or early):

$$\text{Usual Original Time } T_{\text{usual}} = \frac{x}{y - x} \times t \quad (\text{for } y > x)$$
