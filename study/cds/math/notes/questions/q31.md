# CDS Practice Q31: Euclidean Long Division for High-Degree Polynomials

## Question Text

Find the HCF of the polynomials:
$$P(x) = 22x^6 - 78x^5 - 16x^2$$
$$Q(x) = 2x^5 - 78x^4 - 44x^2$$

---

## Detailed Mathematical Solution

### Step 1: Extract Common Monomial Factors First

Before doing long division, extract common monomial factors from both polynomials:
- $P(x) = 2x^2(11x^4 - 39x^3 - 8)$
- $Q(x) = 2x^2(x^4 - 39x - 22)$

The common monomial HCF of the outer terms is $\operatorname{HCF}(2x^2, 2x^2) = 2x^2$.

Now, we only need to find the HCF of the inner polynomials:
$$f(x) = 11x^4 - 39x^3 - 8$$
$$g(x) = x^4 - 39x - 22$$

---

### Step 2: Perform Euclidean Long Division $f(x) \div g(x)$

Divide $11x^4 - 39x^3 - 8$ by $x^4 - 39x - 22$:
- Quotient $= 11$.
- $11 \cdot (x^4 - 39x - 22) = 11x^4 - 429x - 242$.
- Subtracting gives remainder:
  $$\text{Remainder}_1 = -39x^3 + 429x + 234$$

---

### Step 3: Why Do We Strip Intermediate Remainder Scalar Factors ($-39$ and $11$)?

Notice that in $\text{Remainder}_1 = -39x^3 + 429x + 234$, the number **$-39$** is a pure scalar factor:
$$-39x^3 + 429x + 234 = -39(x^3 - 11x - 6)$$

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Dual Reasons Why Intermediate Factors Can Be Ignored**:
> 
> 1. **Complementary Factor Reason (Not a Factor of the Dividend)**:
>    The stripped multiplier ($-39$ or $x^k$) **cannot be a factor of the polynomial we are about to divide ($g(x) = x^4 - 39x - 22$)**, because we ALREADY extracted all common factors in Stage 1! Since $-39$ does NOT divide $g(x)$, it can NEVER be part of the common divisor $\operatorname{HCF}(g(x), R(x))$. Therefore, discarding it loses ZERO common factors.
> 
> 2. **Quotient Adjustment Reason (Absorbed into Quotient)**:
>    Any scalar constant or extraneous $x^k$ present in a remainder can always be **absorbed into the quotient of the subsequent division step** (by scaling quotient terms). Because quotient scaling does not affect whether the final remainder eventually reaches $0$, intermediate multipliers have **zero impact on the final algebraic HCF**.

**Why we discard $-39$**:
1. We **already extracted** all shared scalar and $x^k$ factors in Step 1 ($2x^2$).
2. The stripped factor $-39$ does not divide the target dividend $g(x)$, so it cannot belong to $\operatorname{HCF}(g, R_1)$.
3. Discarding $-39$ keeps the next divisor clean ($x^3 - 11x - 6$) without introducing fractional algebra in subsequent division steps!

---

### Step 4: Next Division Step

Divide $g(x) = x^4 - 39x - 22$ by new divisor $x^3 - 11x - 6$:
- Quotient $= x$.
- $x \cdot (x^3 - 11x - 6) = x^4 - 11x^2 - 6x$.
- Subtracting gives remainder:
  $$\text{Remainder}_2 = 11x^2 - 33x - 22$$

Extract scalar factor $11$:
$$11x^2 - 33x - 22 = 11(x^2 - 3x - 2)$$
Discard scalar $11$ for the same reason (clean polynomial divisor).

---

### Step 5: Final Division Step

Divide $x^3 - 11x - 6$ by $x^2 - 3x - 2$:
- Quotient $= x + 3$.
- $(x + 3)(x^2 - 3x - 2) = x^3 - 11x - 6$.
- Remainder is **$0$**.

Thus, the inner polynomial HCF is $(x^2 - 3x - 2)$.

---

### Final Result

Combine the outer monomial HCF ($2x^2$) with the inner polynomial HCF $(x^2 - 3x - 2)$:
$$\operatorname{HCF}(P(x), Q(x)) = 2x^2(x^2 - 3x - 2)$$

**Correct Answer**: **(b) $2x^2(x^2 - 3x - 2)$**

---

## Key Takeaway & Exam Strategy
- **Step 1**: Always factor out common numeric multipliers and powers of $x$ ($2x^2$) first!
- **Step 2**: During Euclidean long division, **strip away intermediate scalar numbers** (like $-39$ or $11$) from remainders. They do not alter polynomial HCF roots and prevent fractional polynomial division!
