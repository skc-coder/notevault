# CDS PYQ 2013 II Q34: Exponent HCF and Imaginary Roots Trap

## Question Text

The HCF of $(x^4 - y^4)$ and $(x^6 - y^6)$ is:

**Options:**
- (a) $x^2 - y^2$
- (b) $x - y$
- (c) $x^3 - y^3$
- (d) $x^4 - y^4$

---

## Detailed Mathematical Solution

### Method 1: Exponent GCD Theorem (Fastest Method)
For power difference expressions $x^n - y^n$ and $x^m - y^m$:
$$\operatorname{HCF}(x^n - y^n, x^m - y^m) = x^{\operatorname{gcd}(n, m)} - y^{\operatorname{gcd}(n, m)}$$

Here, the exponents are $n = 4$ and $m = 6$:
$$\operatorname{gcd}(4, 6) = 2$$

$$\operatorname{HCF}(x^4 - y^4, x^6 - y^6) = x^2 - y^2$$

---

### Method 2: Complete Factorization over Reals

1. Factorize $x^4 - y^4$:
   $$x^4 - y^4 = (x^2 - y^2)(x^2 + y^2)$$
   $$x^4 - y^4 = (x - y)(x + y)(x^2 + y^2)$$

2. Factorize $x^6 - y^6$:
   $$x^6 - y^6 = (x^3 - y^3)(x^3 + y^3)$$
   $$x^6 - y^6 = (x - y)(x^2 + xy + y^2) \cdot (x + y)(x^2 - xy + y^2)$$

3. Extract Shared Factors:
   - Shared linear factors: $(x - y)$ and $(x + y)$
   - Common quadratic product: $(x - y)(x + y) = x^2 - y^2$

$$\operatorname{HCF} = x^2 - y^2$$

**Correct Answer**: **(a) $x^2 - y^2$**

---

## Critical Trap Analysis: Why Option (d) $x^4 - y^4$ Fails (Imaginary Roots Factorization)

> [!WARNING]
> **Why Option (d) is WRONG & Imaginary Roots Explanation**:
> 
> Option (d) factorizes completely over complex numbers into 4 linear root factors:
> $$x^4 - y^4 = (x - y)(x + y)(x - i y)(x + i y)$$
> 
> 1. **Real Roots ($x = \pm y$)**:
>    - Roots $x = y$ and $x = -y$ correspond to real factors $(x - y)(x + y) = x^2 - y^2$.
>    - These REAL roots ARE shared by $x^6 - y^6$.
> 
> 2. **Imaginary Roots ($x = \pm i y$)**:
>    - The factor $(x^2 + y^2) = (x - i y)(x + i y)$ introduces imaginary roots $x = \pm i y$.
>    - Substituting $x^2 = -y^2$ (imaginary root) into $x^6 - y^6$:
>      $$( -y^2 )^3 - y^6 = -y^6 - y^6 = -2y^6 \neq 0$$
>    - Since $(x^2 + y^2)$ does NOT evaluate to zero for $x^6 - y^6$, the imaginary roots $x = \pm i y$ are **NOT shared**!
> 
> Therefore, $(x^2 + y^2)$ cannot be a common divisor, ruling out Option (d) $x^4 - y^4$. Only $(x^2 - y^2)$ is shared!

---

## Key Takeaway & Exam Strategy
Use $\operatorname{gcd}(\text{exponents})$: $\operatorname{gcd}(4, 6) = 2 \implies x^2 - y^2$. Option (d) fails because its extra factor $(x^2 + y^2)$ contains imaginary roots $x = \pm i y$ that do NOT divide $x^6 - y^6$.
